# Partial-Knowledge Assimilation Concept Map

## Target learner

Default target for difficult Grade 9 / competitive-foundation material:

> a student who already carries roughly half of the topic: some vocabulary, one or two procedures, and fragments of formula memory, but whose connections, recognition, method choice, and transfer are unreliable.

Do not treat this learner as a blank slate. Do not assume remembered formulas imply understanding.

## Governing concept map

```text
WHAT THE STUDENT ALREADY HAS
    |
    v
RECONNECT prior knowledge
    |-- retrieve a familiar example
    |-- expose what is remembered correctly
    |-- expose the missing link
    v
DISCOVER the new relationship
    |-- concrete pattern / contrast / worked fragment
    |-- student notices before teacher names
    v
MAKE SENSE of the structure
    |-- invariant / reason / derivation
    |-- representation changes
    |-- why the tempting wrong model fails
    v
TRY with no full solution
    |-- student writes first move
    |-- teacher/book observes the choice
    v
DIAGNOSE the actual gap
    |-- recognition gap?
    |-- representation gap?
    |-- concept gap?
    |-- algebra/calculation gap?
    |-- condition/domain gap?
    v
FADE assistance
    |-- H3 execution cue
    |-- H2 structural cue
    |-- H1 recognition cue
    |-- H0 independent
    v
ADOPT a reusable internal rule
    |-- "when I see..., I test... because..."
    |-- reject a close wrong method
    v
TRANSFER to a changed surface form
    |-- non-identical numbers
    |-- different representation
    |-- mixed-topic disguise
    |-- source-integrity / boundary case
    v
INDEPENDENT OWNERSHIP
```

## Relationship to SEE -> REALIZE -> UNDERSTAND -> ADOPT

The four-stage mathematics contract remains the macro cognitive spine:

- `SEE` corresponds mainly to `RECONNECT + DISCOVER`;
- `REALIZE` corresponds to naming the hidden relationship/invariant;
- `UNDERSTAND` corresponds to `MAKE SENSE + DIAGNOSE`;
- `ADOPT` is reached only after `TRY + FADE + TRANSFER`.

The eight-step assimilation loop is therefore not a competing pedagogy. It is the operational teaching choreography inside the four-stage contract.

## Mandatory topic concept map before authoring

Before writing prose, draw the topic as a connected graph with these node classes:

1. **PRIOR KNOWLEDGE NODES** — what a 50%-prepared student is likely to know already.
2. **BRIDGE NODES** — the missing connections that convert fragments into a coherent model.
3. **CORE INVARIANT NODES** — the structures worth remembering even if formulas are forgotten.
4. **REPRESENTATION NODES** — equation, factor, graph, table, diagram, words, invariant form, etc.
5. **DECISION BOUNDARIES** — nearby problems that require different methods.
6. **MISCONCEPTION NODES** — plausible wrong models and why they are attractive.
7. **FIRST-MOVE NODES** — the minimum useful line once the structure is recognized.
8. **TRANSFER NODES** — changed-surface problems that prove ownership.
9. **SOURCE-CUSTODY NODES** — PYQ IDs, figure dependence, conflict/bonus status where applicable.

Every major concept must have at least one path:

`PRIOR -> BRIDGE -> INVARIANT -> FIRST MOVE -> TRANSFER`

and at least one competing path:

`VISIBLE CLUE -> TEMPTING WRONG MODEL -> CONTRAST -> CORRECT DECISION`.

## The six-question assimilation test

A concept is not assimilated merely because the student can repeat a worked example. Ask:

1. What did you notice?
2. Why does the method work?
3. What clue would make you think of it?
4. What similar-looking situation would require a different method?
5. Can you write the first two useful lines without help?
6. Can you solve a disguised version?

Interpretation:

- fails 1 -> observation/representation gap;
- fails 2 -> conceptual/reconstruction gap;
- fails 3 -> recognition gap;
- fails 4 -> decision-boundary gap;
- fails 5 -> execution-start gap;
- fails 6 -> transfer gap.

Do not label the concept mastered until all six are credible.

## Hint fading contract

Use staged hints only after an independent attempt.

- `H0 INDEPENDENT` — no hint.
- `H1 RECOGNITION` — point to the visible clue or question type, not the formula.
- `H2 STRUCTURE` — name the invariant/representation to build.
- `H3 EXECUTION` — give the first algebraic relation, not the finished solution.

On repeated practice, remove H3 first, then H2, then H1.

A student who succeeds only with H2/H3 has not reached ADOPT.

## Contrast-pair contract

Close contrasts are mandatory because partial knowledge often fails at method selection.

For every important method include a nearby non-example or competing method. The student must explain both:

- why the correct method fits;
- why the tempting method is inferior or invalid.

The contrast should alter one structural condition at a time where possible.

## Product architecture

### Assimilation Book

Purpose: repair and complete the mental model.

Typical order:

`RECONNECT -> DISCOVER -> MAKE SENSE -> TRY -> DIAGNOSE -> FADE -> ADOPT -> TRANSFER`

Include derivations, commentary on expert noticing, misconception repair, contrast pairs, guided attempts, and transfer.

### First-Step Reference

Purpose: compress already-assimilated understanding for revision and fast recognition.

Use:

`SEE -> REALIZE -> WRITE -> CHOOSE -> CHECK`

Include atlas, phrase decoder, decision tree, first-step cards, traps, recognition-only drills, and source-to-mechanism map.

**Rule:** the First-Step Reference may compress understanding but must not be the sole teaching layer for a difficult concept.

### Mixed assessment

Purpose: prove independent method selection under disguise.

No chapter labels, no method headings, no first-move hints in the student paper.

## Teacher-authoring question

Before every page ask:

> If the learner understands about half of this already, does this page reveal the missing connection, or does it merely repeat information they probably know?

If it only repeats, compress it or convert it into a diagnostic/reconnect step.
