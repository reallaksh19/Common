# Core teaching contract

## Concept and syllabus map

For each concept record: stable ID; phenomenon; canonical claim; prerequisites in Physics, language and mathematics; observable learning outcome; necessary depiction; reference frame/sign convention; equation passport; wrong model; guided attempt; independent transfer; repair route; source locator; grade boundary.

Break a topic where reasoning changes, not at convenient page counts. Distance/displacement needs position, route length and endpoint change before formulas. Graph area needs axes, duration, signed velocity, constant-interval movement and area before combining regions.

| Concept | Depiction | Evidence beyond a number |
|---|---|---|
| Position | Number line, zero, positive direction | Explain how a positive position can accompany leftward movement |
| Distance | Ordered route arrows and leg lengths | Explain why returning does not erase walking |
| Displacement | Distinct start-to-finish arrow | Reconstruct final minus initial from a nonzero start |
| Signed v–t area | Axes, rectangle and one-second cells | Explain metre units and negative-region meaning |
| Distance from v–t | Zero line and separate area regions | Construct zero displacement with positive distance |

Grades 9–11 are an authoring range, not permission to mix all topics. Record board, jurisdiction, year and source edition. If unknown, use “selected foundation topics”; do not claim syllabus completeness. Label concepts REQUIRED_IN_REQUESTED_SOURCE, PREREQUISITE_REPAIR, OPTIONAL_EXTENSION or OUT_OF_SCOPE. Cite the first category to a supplied or verified source. Do not invent a Grade 10 Motion curriculum between Grades 9 and 11. Signed coordinates and graph slope are not intrinsically Grade 11; map their treatment to the actual source.

For other Physics branches choose a representation appropriate to the reasoning: force diagrams for interactions, energy stores/transfers for energy, circuit structure and readings for electricity, labelled rays for optics, particles/waves for oscillations. Verify grade placement and implement renderer support before promising those outputs.

## Chalkboard sequence

1. Choose a familiar, mechanically faithful situation. Walking along a corridor can expose one-dimensional motion without decorative clip art.
2. Show the relationship to notice. Mark origin, start, finish, units and positive direction. Align arrows to numerical positions; make route order visible.
3. Ask the physical question: “How much ground?” versus “What changed from the start?”
4. Say the relation in ordinary language; then define symbols. Explain delta as change and magnitude as size without the directional sign.
5. Build each operation from the picture. Subtraction compares endpoints. Multiplication repeats constant change. The half in triangular area comes from geometry.
6. State validity and a check. Constant velocity gives a rectangle. Use triangular area only when the region bounded by the graph and zero line really is a triangle. A sloping segment away from zero may form a trapezium. Split distance calculations at sign changes.
7. Work an example with model choice, calculation, units/sign and physical check.
8. Fade one support at a time: full number line → learner marks one coordinate → partial route table → whole independent problem. Do not jump from a complete worked solution to a multi-part nonzero-origin task for B30.
9. Give a genuinely new independent task. Do not reuse every number and endpoint from the worked example under an “independent” badge.
10. Link the error to a visual repair and a retry. If drawing is the repair, show a completed correct drawing in the final solution.

## Band contract

| Stage | B30 | B80 | B90 minimum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Encounter | Stable story, labelled diagram, first-use words | Compact depiction and contrast | Decisive depiction and conditions |
| Mathematics | Count, say, build, compress | Reconstruct with fewer arithmetic steps | Justify and inspect model limits |
| Guided | Full → one-step → partially completed → independent | Partial model or first-step cue | Modelling check when needed |
| Independent | Near transfer then unfamiliar context | Origin/sign change, counterexample, representation transfer | Unfamiliar model choice or limiting case |
| Repair | Return to picture and spoken meaning | Target the assumption or representation | Diagnose model failure rather than repeat arithmetic |

Do not turn B30 into vocabulary-only content or B90 into a formula sheet. Keep concept outcomes fixed while varying support. Percent labels are hypotheses, not a validated placement instrument. Use actual explanations and representation choices to adjust teaching.

## Equation passport

Require system/frame; linked depiction; symbol meanings and units; spoken interpretation; origin of signs/operations/factors; reconstruction; assumptions; unit/sign/special-case checks; prediction; transfer.

For `Δx = v Δt`: each second contributes the same signed movement, so multiply by elapsed time. Δt is elapsed time, not the final clock reading. Units `(m/s) × s = m`. The rectangle requires constant velocity. At v = 0, change is zero. Doubling duration doubles displacement only while that same velocity persists.

Distinguish graph height, slope and area explicitly. On v–t axes they give velocity, rate of velocity change and displacement. A graph is not the road or the trajectory through space. Touching zero is not enough to prove reversal; velocity must change sign.

## Questions, hints and appendices

Give every question one primary concept. Secondary dependencies must not inflate per-concept scores. If scoring a mixed question, allocate points by criterion; do not infer mastery from a raw total.

For the two-topic pilot include direct application, nonzero origin, misconception/counterexample, representation translation and unfamiliar transfer. Retain some shared anchor tasks between bands. These are design probes, not empirical diagnostic norms.

Separate arithmetic demand from model-selection demand. Use learner task badges Apply, Explain, Compare, Connect and Transfer. Do not invent mastery or exam-difficulty badges.

Appendix A is independently attemptable. Reproduce all data, graphs/tables/options the prompt depends on. Leave a drawing absent only when constructing it is the deliberate task. Give usable space.

Appendix B is a printable one-page handout with topic names, essential diagrams, plain-language meaning, symbols, units, conditions and a worked memory anchor. Do not include unique answers to independent questions. All practice solutions follow it at the very end.

H1 identifies the decisive clue, H2 proposes a representation/model, H3 gives the first executable line. They must be different, optional and free of final-answer leakage. Solutions repeat enough data and representations to be self-contained, explain why, show a usable method, state the answer/check and the transferable idea, then return to the question.

## Multiple review passes

1. Source/scope: source roles, claims, denominators, explicit rewrites.
2. Physics teacher: justification, signs, frame, units, limiting cases, shape assumptions.
3. B30 walkthrough: first unexplained word/operation; missing picture; gap between worked and faded tasks.
4. B80/B90 walkthrough: retained depiction; model choice and transfer rather than larger numbers.
5. Assessment/repair: optional progressive hints, no answer leakage, complete solutions and useful repair.
6. Publication: diagram/prose grouping, font sizes, print navigation, appendix order and actual rendered pages.
7. Recheck: verify affected pages and freeze final model/PDF hashes after revisions.

Record reviewer role, location, observation, learner consequence, revision and recheck. Label simulations honestly. Actual classroom effectiveness requires actual learner work, explanations, hint use and delayed transfer; no schema check proves it.
