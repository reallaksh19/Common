# Redox concept review map

The chapter follows prerequisite order rather than ExamSIDE frequency.

| Stage | Main concept | Representation / helper | Reviewer probe |
|---|---|---|---|
| ST01 | Oxidation number | formula anatomy, rule-priority ladder, charge-balance model, average-vs-actual diagram | Can a learner explain why the sum equals ion charge and why peroxide oxygen is exceptional? |
| ST02 | Oxidation / reduction | electron tokens and before→after ON lanes | Can the learner explain why losing negative electrons makes ON increase? |
| ST03 | Oxidising / reducing agents | SELF vs OTHER and electron-flow frame | Can the learner derive “oxidised species = reducing agent” without rote reversal? |
| ST04 | Redox / non-redox | redox fingerprint and UP+DOWN check | Can the learner reject O₂→O₃ and CaO+H₂O as redox without using superficial cues? |
| ST05 | Redox types | topology helpers: combine/split/displace, MIDDLE→LOW+HIGH, LOW+HIGH→MIDDLE | Can the learner distinguish disproportionation from comproportionation and type from redox status? |
| ST06 | Integrated pipeline | SELECT→ASSIGN→TRACK→CLASSIFY→ATTACH→NAME→CHECK | Can the learner stop at the minimum justified stage for the question asked? |

## High-risk misconceptions under review

1. Subscripts or ion charges are oxidation numbers.
2. Oxygen is always −2; hydrogen is always +1.
3. Fractional average oxidation number means every atom has that value.
4. Electron loss should make oxidation number decrease.
5. The oxidising agent is itself oxidised.
6. A familiar oxidant always acts as OA in every equation.
7. Visible change, oxygen, gas formation, combination or decomposition proves redox.
8. Disproportionation means only “one reactant gives multiple products.”
9. Comproportionation is any reaction where the same element appears twice.

For each misconception, review whether the book states the wrong model, repairs it causally and gives a retry opportunity.
