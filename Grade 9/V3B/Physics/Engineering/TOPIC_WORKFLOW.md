# Topic setup, extension and controlled depth

Before any topic-specific example, apply the [V3B production blueprint](../Blueprint/V3B-Production-Blueprint.md) and [generic work order](../Blueprint/V3B-Agent-Execution.md). Thermodynamics and Gravity below are illustrative bindings, not universal pipeline branches. See the acceptance ledger for enforcement still pending.

## Owner-visible setup before authoring

A new topic begins with a short Topic Contract, not a manuscript. The owner sees:
topic and grade/exam scope; requested Cores; included/excluded subtopics; intrinsic badges and reasons; Core2 knowledge input or waiver if applicable; source state; gate dependencies; missing prerequisites; research tasks; evidence and review status.

Use five visibly distinct readiness columns:
1. Gate specified — requirements exist.
2. Machine checked — IDs, dependencies and mandatory bindings pass.
3. Subject reviewed — an independent reviewer has checked the actual Physics and completeness.
4. Material realized — explanations, equations, visuals, examples and answers exist at exact locations.
5. Learner release — question custody, learner controls, visual review and owner release are satisfied.

No aggregate green light may hide a red column. UNKNOWN and NOT_RUN are visible values, never zero or PASS. Teacher/owner detail belongs on the board; learners see meaningful concepts, prerequisites, source labels and help links.

## Thermodynamics, systematically

The supplied word “Thermodynamics” is a topic candidate, not a complete scope. Build the school-level map first:
temperature/equilibrium; systems and state; heat/work/internal energy; first law; p-V processes; ideal-gas assumptions; heat capacities where authorized; cycles; second law/entropy and engines where authorized. This proof implements the energy-transfer/first-law/path-work slice. The other rows stay explicitly outside the proof and are not reported as complete.

For each subtopic:
- inventory source concepts, data, equations, examples, question numbers, figures and conditions;
- compare source extraction with original pages before declaring the inventory complete;
- separate source-derived facts, standard model assumptions, authoring recommendations and unresolved claims;
- identify irreducible reasoning transitions and at least one plausible wrong model;
- bind equations to symbols, conditions and diagrams;
- specify independent checks and counterexamples;
- freeze an obligation baseline before the lesson author receives it;
- compile prerequisite closure and a one-to-three-subtopic work bundle;
- author only after the applicable gate is structurally complete and independently reviewed, or label the output a non-release design preview.

The gate need not be independently reinvented for every topic. Reuse an exact existing gate when meaning and assumptions match; bind it to the new bucket. Reuse a role specification freely. Reuse an accepted artifact only when source, gate, purpose and relevant learner-control versions match. Author and independent reviewer may not be the same instance.

## What happens when a subtopic is added

Example: add thermodynamic cycles to the existing first-law topic.

1. Append a stable PHY-TH-CYCLE identity and its dependencies; do not rename the first-law gate.
2. Add cycle obligations: state closure, Delta U=0, net heat/net work relation, oriented p-V loop, and a reversed-loop counterexample.
3. Add the new gate to the topic's selected gates. Recompute transitive dependencies and exact obligation IDs.
4. Version the topic binding. Record the previous binding and added/changed/deleted IDs.
5. Invalidate only products depending on changed objects, plus their downstream receipts. Independent first-law material can remain accepted if unchanged and still sufficient; cycle questions need new teaching evidence.
6. If a shared prerequisite changes meaning, invalidate every dependent bucket transitively.
7. Show the owner the impact: retained artifacts, new authoring, reviews to repeat, stale questions and publication status.
8. Commit the change receipt before accepting work returned under the old version.

Deleting a gate with dependents blocks. Prerequisite cycles block. Unknown dependencies block. Editing an expected-obligation list to silence a failure is an authority change requiring baseline review, never a normal author repair.

Depth expansion inside the same concept uses a new profile/binding version. A genuinely new concept receives a new gate. A new topic may reuse gates from another topic through explicit dependencies. A bundle limit does not limit the number of learning atoms or divide a 30-page bucket into artificially independent concepts.

## Research-depth Gravity

“Research level” is a depth request and must identify its target. It might mean a rigorous school model with derivations and approximation analysis, a guided reading of research papers, or independent research methodology. These are different deliverables.

The implemented example adopts a bounded target: investigate when constant-g gravity is a sufficient approximation, derive the error analytically, and design a reproducible comparison. It does not claim to teach all gravity or produce new science.

The Gravity overlay retains the school Newtonian gates and adds:
- explicit model scope (spherical source, exterior field, test particle, nonrelativistic regime);
- potential/field relation and reference convention;
- exact-versus-approximate comparison and an error definition;
- research question, assumptions, derivation, reproducible calculation, falsifier and limitations;
- provenance class for every literature claim;
- a route into advanced relativity with real prerequisite holds.

General relativity and gravitational-wave research are a separate advanced branch. Differential geometry, tensor methods and requisite mathematical physics cannot be waived by changing a badge to HARD. A conceptual bridge can be authored while derivation-level release stays blocked. MIT 8.962 is a graduate course; its published prerequisites inform the gap map, not a claim that Grade9 learners already possess them.

Research-depth material is segregated from exam scope. No research equation enters Core2A/2B by implication. An authorized scope extension must update teaching requirements and the legal question pool. Core1A/1B still use intrinsic badges; research depth is an additional profile, not a fourth difficulty badge. Core2A/2B still require knowledge or an explicit owner waiver for actual personalized generation.

## Gate and agent lifecycle

DRAFT → STRUCTURE_CHECKED → SUBJECT_REVIEW_PENDING → AUTHORING_READY → REALIZATION_REVIEW → RELEASE_REVIEW.

A held source, unresolved model or missing prerequisite stays attached throughout. A machine can derive structural state; it cannot manufacture subject approval. A design preview may exist before full release, with prominent status and no production-ready claim.

At each handoff carry exact topic/gate/baseline/source versions, inventory dispositions, scientific reasoning, unresolved obligations, artifact locations, review provenance and the exact next action. The receiver acknowledges the manifest and demonstrates the critical invariant in its own derivation. A returned packet using a stale gate version is rejected before its content can replace accepted work.

A fresh reviewer must inspect original sources and actual mathematical/diagram content. Fresh context is a contamination control, not proof of expertise. Research reviewers require expertise in the advanced branch being assessed.

## AUTO boundary

AUTO may inventory, research, propose gates, create specimens, execute checks, repair defects and prepare the draft. It may not invent owner waivers, independent approval, learner observations, source question numbers or merge authority. It proceeds with non-release previews where those inputs are missing, completing all useful reviewable work.
