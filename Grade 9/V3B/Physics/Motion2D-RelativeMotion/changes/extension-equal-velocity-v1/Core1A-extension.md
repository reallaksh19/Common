# Core 1A extension — B05E equal velocity, different initial position

<a id="b05e-worked-example"></a>
## Worked example: zero relative velocity does not erase a gap

Take +x east. At `t=0`, A is at `x_A0=−5 m` and B is at `x_B0=25 m`. Both move east at `+4 m/s` in the ground frame.

1. Ground-frame positions are `x_A=−5+4t` and `x_B=25+4t`.
2. Relative velocity is `v_A/B=4−4=0 m/s`.
3. Initial relative position is `x_A/B(0)=−5−25=−30 m`.
4. Using `x_A/B(t)=x_A/B(0)+v_A/B t`, we get `x_A/B(t)=−30 m` for every t.
5. At 10 s, A is at 35 m and B at 65 m: the separation is still 30 m.

The minus sign in `x_A/B=−30 m` means A is west of B. The separation magnitude is 30 m. Neither object is stationary in the ground frame; both move east at 4 m/s.

**Misconception repair.** `v_A/B=0` says “A's position relative to B is not changing.” It does not say “A and B occupy the same position.” Co-location would additionally require `x_A/B=0`.

<a id="b05e-check-answer"></a>
**Checkpoint — EXT-C1A-Q01.** C starts 18 m west of D; both move west at 6 m/s. After 5 s, what is their separation?  
**Answer:** 18 m. Their equal velocities give zero relative velocity, so the nonzero initial relative position stays constant.
