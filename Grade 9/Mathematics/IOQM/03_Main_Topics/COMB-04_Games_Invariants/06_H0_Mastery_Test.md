# Mixed Mastery Test

Solve each problem from scratch. Justify every claim that is needed for the conclusion.

1. Nine lamps are arranged in a row. A legal move toggles exactly four lamps of your choice. Initially exactly three lamps are on. Can all lamps be turned off?

2. A state is an integer triple. A legal move adds a permutation of `(2,-1,-1)`. Can `(4,4,4)` reach `(5,4,3)`?

3. Six lamps lie on a cycle. A legal move toggles three consecutive lamps. Starting from all lamps off, can a configuration with exactly one lamp on be reached?

4. A pair `(a,b)` consists of positive unequal integers. A move replaces the larger entry by the positive difference of the entries. Prove that the process cannot continue forever.

5. A heap contains 2026 stones. Two players alternate removing 1 or 2 stones. The player who removes the last stone wins. Determine the winner under optimal play and give a strategy.

6. A heap contains 20 stones. Two players alternate removing 1, 3, or 4 stones. The player who removes the last stone wins. Determine the winner under optimal play and justify the classification you use.

7. Pair the integers `1,2,...,10` into five pairs so that the product of the five pair sums is a perfect square. Exhibit such a pairing and verify the product.

8. Two heaps contain 7 and 10 stones. Two players alternate; each move removes exactly one stone from one nonempty heap. The player making the last move wins. Determine the winner and justify your answer.

9. A proposed proof says: “The quantity `M` never increases after a legal move, so the process must terminate.” Is that argument valid in general? Give a precise reason or counterexample.

10. A parameterized existence problem has a proved invariant showing that one residue class of parameters is impossible. Several examples are constructed in the remaining classes. Explain exactly what is still required before an if-and-only-if classification is proved.
