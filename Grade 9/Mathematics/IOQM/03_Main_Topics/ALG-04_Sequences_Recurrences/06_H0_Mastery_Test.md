# ALG-04 — H0 Mastery Test

No default hints or method labels.

1. The partial sums of a sequence are `S_n=4n^2+n`. Find `a_n`.
2. A sequence satisfies `a_{n+2}=5a_{n+1}-4a_n`, `a_1=2`, `a_2=5`. Find `a_8` efficiently.
3. Evaluate `sum_{k=1}^{50} 1/[k(k+1)]`.
4. Every 6-term sum of a sequence is greater than the preceding 6-term sum. State and prove the corresponding termwise inequality.
5. Every 4-term sum of a sequence is equal to the preceding 4-term sum. Prove the sequence is periodic with period dividing 4.
6. Determine whether `1,4,9,16,25,...` is AP or GP; then identify a transformed sequence with a simpler pattern.
7. Let `a_1=3,a_2=7` and `a_{n+2}=3a_{n+1}-2a_n`. Find `a_{20}` without listing 20 terms.
8. Evaluate `sum_{k=1}^{n} 1/[(2k-1)(2k+1)]`.
9. A recurrence arose from counting paths in a grid. Explain which part belongs to ALG-04 and which part belongs to COMB-03.
10. Give one example where a high-index recurrence problem is best solved by an invariant or transformed sequence rather than direct iteration, and justify the transformation.

For two items, state the clue that selected your first move.
