# COMB-05 - Mixed Mastery Test

No method labels or default hints are supplied on the first attempt.

1. Twenty integers are written on a board. Prove that two have the same remainder when divided by 19.
2. Thirty-seven objects are distributed among 9 containers. Prove that one container receives at least 5 objects.
3. Choose 8 integers from `{1,2,...,14}`. Prove that two sum to 15.
4. Five points lie in a unit square. Prove that two are at distance at most `sqrt(2)/2`.
5. Choose 51 distinct integers from `{1,...,100}`. Prove that two are consecutive.
6. Among any 6 people, prove that there are three mutual acquaintances or three mutual strangers.
7. A finite nonempty set of positive real numbers is closed under replacing two unequal members by their geometric mean. Explain why choosing a smallest member is not, by itself, a useful extremal step; identify the missing contradiction mechanism.
8. A problem asks for the exact number of arrangements satisfying at least one of three overlapping restrictions. Explain why a pigeonhole proof cannot replace overlap-corrected counting.
9. A problem asks for a lower bound on a real expression subject to `xy=1`. Explain why selecting the smallest of finitely many objects is not the right model.
10. Historical-anchor audit: among all increasing quadruples from `{1,...,20}`, balanced means `a+c=b+d`. Determine the number of balanced quadruples.
11. Historical-anchor consequence: if 4411 such quadruples are selected, what minimum number must be balanced?
12. Transfer: 17 points lie in a square of side 2. Prove that two points are at distance at most `sqrt(2)`.
