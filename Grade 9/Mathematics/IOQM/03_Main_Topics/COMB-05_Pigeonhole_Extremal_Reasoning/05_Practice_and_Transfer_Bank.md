# COMB-05 - Practice and Transfer Bank

## F0 - reconnect
1. Put 25 balls into 6 boxes. Prove that one box contains at least 5 balls.
2. Among 8 integers, prove that two have the same remainder modulo 7.

## F1 - direct and generalized pigeonhole
3. Among 101 integers, prove that two have a difference divisible by 100.
4. Forty students are assigned to 12 months by birth month. Prove that some month has at least 4 birthdays.
5. Choose 11 integers from `{1,...,20}`. Prove that two are consecutive.

## F2 - geometric and number-theoretic surfaces
6. Five points lie in a unit square. Prove that two are at distance at most `sqrt(2)/2`.
7. Ten points lie in an equilateral triangle of side 1. Prove that two are at distance at most `1/3`.
8. Choose 7 integers from `{1,...,12}`. Prove that two have sum 13.
9. Among any 5 integers, prove that two have a difference divisible by 4.

## F3 - extremal choice
10. In any finite set of at least two distinct real numbers, choose the closest pair. Explain why no third selected point can lie strictly between them at smaller distance from one endpoint.
11. A finite graph has at least one edge. Choose a vertex of largest degree. State the immediate degree comparison available against every other vertex.
12. A finite family of nonempty sets is partially ordered by inclusion. Choose a set of smallest cardinality. Explain what would contradict this choice.

## F4 - changed-surface transfer
13. Fifty-one distinct integers are selected from `{1,...,100}`. Prove that two are consecutive. Then explain why exact enumeration of all 51-subsets is irrelevant.
14. Twenty-five points lie in a square of side 2. Prove that two are at distance at most `sqrt(2)/2`.
15. A party has 6 people. Prove that there are three mutual acquaintances or three mutual strangers.
16. A collection of 4411 increasing quadruples `(a,b,d,c)` is chosen from `1<=a<b<d<c<=20`. Call a quadruple balanced when `a+c=b+d`. Prove that at least 91 chosen quadruples are balanced.

## Why-not / verification
17. Why does an average of 3.2 objects per box not by itself prove a particular box has exactly 4 objects?
18. Why is inclusion-exclusion oversized for: 'among 13 integers, two differ by a multiple of 12'?
19. Why is AM-GM not the canonical first move in a finite configuration problem that says 'choose the shortest edge and derive a crossing contradiction'?
20. In a geometric pigeonhole proof, why must the diameter of each cell be checked rather than only its area?
