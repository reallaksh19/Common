# NT-01 — Practice and Transfer Bank

All non-historical items are author-created.

## F0 Foundation

1. State whether `18|234` and justify with an integer quotient.
2. Compute `gcd(84,126)` and `lcm(84,126)`.
3. Verify `gcd(18,24)lcm(18,24)=18*24`.

## F1 Direct

4. Find the greatest integer dividing 155 and 239 with the same remainder.
5. Find the least positive integer divisible by 16, 20 and 24.
6. Use Euclid to compute `gcd(2025,748)`.

## F2 Standard

7. Find the greatest integer that leaves the same remainder on dividing 437, 581 and 725.
8. Find the least `N>9` such that `N` leaves remainder 9 on division by 12, 15 and 20.
9. If `gcd(a,b)=12` and `lcm(a,b)=420`, determine `ab`.

## F3 Disguised

10. An integer `d>1` divides both `7x+5y` and `3x+2y`. Prove `d` divides a simple linear combination of `x,y` and use it to constrain possible `d` when `x,y` are coprime.
11. Three machines reset every 18, 24 and 40 minutes. They reset together at 09:00. When next?
12. Positive integers `a,b` have gcd 15 and lcm 900. Write `a=15u, b=15v`, derive the condition on `uv`, then list possible unordered coprime pairs `(u,v)`.

## F4 Preliminary-style

13. Find the greatest `d` such that 1001, 1457 and 1913 leave the same remainder upon division by `d`.
14. Find the least positive integer `N` such that `N+5` is divisible by 18, 24 and 30.
15. Suppose `d` divides `4n+7` and `7n+13`. Show that `d` must divide a fixed integer independent of `n`, and determine all possible positive `d`.

## Transfer prompts

16. Re-express “`a` and `b` have the same remainder modulo `d`” without using congruence notation.
17. Explain why Euclid and same-remainder subtraction are manifestations of the same invariant.
18. Give one situation where lcm is tempting but gcd is correct, and explain the decision boundary.
