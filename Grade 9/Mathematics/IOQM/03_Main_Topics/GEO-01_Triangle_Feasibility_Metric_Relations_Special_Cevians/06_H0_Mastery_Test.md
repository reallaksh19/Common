# H0 Mastery Test — GEO-01

Status: `UNHINTED_MIXED_MASTERY_V1`

Attempt all items **without labels, hints, theorem names in the margin, or method prompts**. Show the first structural line before sustained calculation.

## M1
Two sides of a triangle are `12` and `20`. How many integer values can the third side have?

## M2
The set is

`n, n+4, n+8, ..., n+24`.

Three **distinct** values are selected. Find the least positive integer `n` such that every selected triple forms an acute triangle.

## M3
In triangle `ABC`, point `D` lies on `BC` and `BD=DC`. No perpendicular or equal-angle information is stated. Classify `AD` as precisely as the data allow, and list one property you are **not** allowed to assume.

## M4
In triangle `ABC`, `AB=10`, `AC=14`, `BC=12`, and `M` is the midpoint of `BC`. Find `AM^2`.

## M5
In triangle `ABC`, `AD` bisects `angle A`, `AB:AC=7:5`, and `BC=36`. Find `BD` and `DC`.

## M6
In triangle `ABC`, `AB=15`, `AC=13`, `BC=14`. Point `D` lies on `BC` with `BD=5`, `DC=9`. Find `AD^2`.

## M7
A right triangle has an altitude to the hypotenuse that divides the hypotenuse into segments `9` and `16`. Find the altitude, hypotenuse, and both legs.

## M8
For a triangle, the exradii opposite sides `a,b,c` are respectively `6,3,2`. Reconstruct `a,b,c`.

## M9
How many non-congruent isosceles triangles with integer side lengths have perimeter `25`?

## M10 — WHY NOT
A diagram shows a cevian `AD` that visually appears perpendicular to `BC` and also appears to bisect `angle A`. The statement gives only `BD:DC=2:1`. A student uses both altitude and angle-bisector identities. Explain why this is invalid, and state the strongest legal classification of `AD` before any further proof.
