# First-Step Reference — Circles, Cyclicity and Tangency

Use this only after you understand the ideas. It is a **routing sheet**, not a theorem catalogue.

## The four-question start

Before calculation, ask:

1. **What circle structure is stated or proved?**
2. **Which points/lines belong to that structure?**
3. **What is the shortest legal relation?**
4. **Would coordinates be cheaper after the structure is identified?**

---

## If you see the same chord

For points on one circle:

- central angle on chord `AB` = twice an inscribed angle on chord `AB`;
- inscribed angles subtending the same chord from the same segment are equal;
- a diameter subtends a right angle.

**First line:** name the shared chord before writing an angle equation.

---

## If four points may be cyclic

Use cyclic relations only after cyclicity is stated or proved.

Fast proof routes:

- opposite angles sum to `180°`;
- two suitable equal angles subtend the same segment.

Once cyclic:

- opposite angles are supplementary;
- same-chord angle relations become available.

**Contrast:** generic quadrilateral gives only total angle sum `360°`.

---

## If a tangent appears

At tangent point `T` with centre `O`:

`OT ⟂ tangent at T`.

From common external point `P`, tangent lengths to the same circle are equal:

`PA=PB`.

**First line:** mark the contact point and the radius perpendicular.

---

## If tangent + chord appear

Test alternate segment before a long angle chase.

If tangent at `A` meets chord `AB`, then the tangent-chord angle equals the inscribed angle subtending `AB` in the opposite segment.

**First line:** write the chord whose endpoints both angles see.

---

## If products of lengths appear

Identify the **power point `P`** first.

- chords `AB,CD` intersect inside at `P`:
  `PA·PB=PC·PD`;
- two secants from outside `P`:
  `PA·PB=PC·PD` with near/far points ordered from `P`;
- tangent `PT` and secant `PAB`:
  `PT²=PA·PB`.

**First line:** label the complete lines through `P`; do not multiply visually nearby segments.

---

## If two circles intersect

Their line of centres is perpendicular to their common chord.

**First line:** identify the two centres and the common chord before writing metric equations.

---

## Synthetic or coordinates?

### Prefer synthetic first when
- the target is an angle;
- cyclicity or tangency gives a one- or two-step relation;
- one power equation closes the length target.

### Test coordinates when
- there is a right triangle, square or rectangle;
- symmetry gives a natural axis;
- lengths are numerical;
- the circle condition becomes one simple equation.

**Rule:** circle structure first, representation second.

---

## Seven danger signals

1. “It looks cyclic.” -> prove cyclicity.
2. “It looks tangent.” -> use stated/proved tangency only.
3. “Opposite angles supplementary.” -> only after cyclicity.
4. “Those tangent lengths look equal.” -> same external point, same circle.
5. “Multiply these four lengths.” -> identify one power point first.
6. “Circle problem means synthetic.” -> compare coordinate cost.
7. “Coordinates always work.” -> test a one-step circle relation first.
