# Practice and Transfer Bank

Try each stage without looking ahead. The support fades as you move down the page.

<!-- F0 -->
## Reconnect

1. A central angle subtending chord `AB` is `128°`. Find an inscribed angle subtending the same chord from the corresponding arc.

2. `ABCD` is cyclic and `∠A=104°`. Find `∠C`.

3. A line is tangent at `T` to a circle with centre `O`. Find the angle between `OT` and the tangent.

4. From external point `P`, tangents `PA,PB` touch the same circle. If `PA=13`, find `PB`.

<!-- F1 -->
## Structure with support

5. Points `A,B,C,D` lie on one circle. Angles `∠ACB` and `∠ADB` subtend chord `AB` from the same segment. If `∠ACB=37°`, find `∠ADB`.

6. A convex quadrilateral has opposite angles `72°` and `108°`. What circle-structure conclusion is available?

7. `AB` is a diameter of a circle of length `10`, and `C` lies on the circle. If `AC=6`, find `BC`.

8. From external point `P`, `PT` is tangent and secant `PAB` meets the circle at `A` then `B`. If `PT=12` and `PA=8`, find `PB`.

<!-- F2 -->
## Reduced support

9. Chords `AB` and `CD` intersect at `P` inside a circle. If `PA=3`, `PB=12` and `PC=4`, find `PD`.

10. Two secants from external point `P` meet the circle in order `P-A-B` and `P-C-D`. If `PA=5`, `PB=20` and `PC=4`, find `PD`.

11. A tangent at `A` forms an angle of `48°` with chord `AB`. Point `C` lies on the opposite arc. Find `∠ACB`.

12. From point `P`, tangents touch a circle at `A,B`. If `PA=10` and `AB=12`, find the perimeter of triangle `PAB`.

<!-- F3 -->
## Mixed independent

13. Prove that tangent segments from the same external point to a circle have equal lengths. Your proof must identify the two right triangles used.

14. In a convex quadrilateral `ABCD`, suppose `∠ABD=∠ACD`. Explain why this is a cyclicity signal and state the conclusion you would justify before using any cyclic-quadrilateral theorem.

15. Two circles intersect at `A,B` and have centres `O1,O2`. A line through `A` is also perpendicular to `AB`. State the direction relation this gives with `O1O2`, and explain why.

16. A problem contains a rectangle, two side points, two equal-length conditions and a concyclicity condition, and asks for a side ratio. Explain why a coordinate placement may be cheaper than a long synthetic chase, but name the circle condition that must still be imposed explicitly.

## Transfer

17. Historical anchor — symmetric isosceles circumcircle.

An isosceles triangle has sides `20,20,30`. The altitude from the apex is bisected at `M`. A chord of the circumcircle through `M` is parallel to the base. Determine the chord length.

18. Historical anchor — square/right-triangle/circle metric.

A right triangle has legs `1` and `2`. A square sits on the longer leg, one upper vertex lies on the hypotenuse, and the other upper vertex lies on the circle centred at the remaining acute vertex and passing through the right-angle vertex. If the square area is `m/n` in lowest terms, determine `m+n`.

19. Historical anchor — rectangle plus cyclicity.

A rectangle has points `M,N` on two adjacent sides with `MC=CD`, `MD=MN`, and `C,D,M,N` concyclic. Under the canonical non-degenerate reading, `(AB/BC)^2=m/n` in lowest terms. Determine `m+n`.

20. Historical anchor — internally tangent circles.

A circle of radius `10` contains two circles tangent to it internally. The two inner circles intersect at `A,B`, and the radius from the large centre to `A` is perpendicular to the common chord `AB`. Determine the sum of the two inner radii.

21. Historical anchor — two circumcentres in a unit square.

In a unit square, points `M,N` lie on consecutive sides near the same vertex, with the perimeter of the small corner triangle equal to `2`. Let `O` be the circumcentre of one triangle involving `M,A,N`, and `P` the circumcentre of the triangle involving `M,O,N`. If `(OP/OA)^2=m/n` in lowest terms, determine `m+n`.

22. Author-created mixed power transfer.

From external point `P`, tangent `PT` and two secants `PAB`, `PCD` meet the same circle. Suppose `PA=4`, `PB=9` and `PC=6`. Determine both `PD` and `PT`, and state the single invariant that makes the two answers agree.
