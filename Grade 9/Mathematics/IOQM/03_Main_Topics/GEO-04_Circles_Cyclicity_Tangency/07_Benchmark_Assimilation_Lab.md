# Benchmark Assimilation Lab — Circles, Cyclicity and Tangency

Use this after the Assimilation Book and before the final mastery test. The aim is to test whether you can **recognize and justify circle structure before selecting a theorem**.

Learning loop:

`RECONNECT -> DIAGNOSE -> ADOPT -> TRANSFER`

## A. RECONNECT — no notes

Write only the first useful relation unless a short explanation is requested.

1. What must be checked before using opposite supplementary angles in a quadrilateral?
2. If two inscribed angles see the same chord from the same segment, what relation do you test first?
3. What local right angle comes from a stated tangent?
4. What must be common before two tangent segments can be declared equal?
5. In a tangent-chord configuration, what geometric object links the tangent angle to an inscribed angle?
6. Before writing a power product, what point must be identified?
7. What relation connects the centres of two intersecting circles to their common chord?
8. When should coordinates be tested instead of extending a synthetic angle chase?

Diagnostic interpretation:

- misses 1-2 -> cyclicity/same-chord recognition is weak;
- misses 3-5 -> tangency/alternate-segment bridge is weak;
- misses 6 -> power-point ownership is weak;
- misses 7 -> intersecting-circle structure is weak;
- misses 8 -> representation-selection boundary is weak.

## B. Error laboratory

For each proposed solution, identify the first invalid or inefficient move and repair it.

### Error 1 — cyclic from appearance

Claim: “The four points look as if they lie on one circle, so opposite angles sum to `180°`.”

Repair target: state what must be proved or given first.

### Error 2 — tangent from appearance

Claim: “The line touches the circle in the drawing, so the radius is perpendicular to it.”

Repair target: require stated tangency or a valid proof of tangency.

### Error 3 — wrong power pairing

Claim: “Two chords cross at `P`, so multiply the two shortest pieces and set that equal to the product of the two longest pieces.”

Repair target: pair the two segments on each complete line through `P`.

### Error 4 — wrong secant length

Claim: “For an external secant, power is outside piece times inside piece.”

Repair target: use distance from the external point to the near intersection times distance from the external point to the far intersection.

### Error 5 — theorem search before structure

Claim: “There is a circle, so list all circle theorems and see which one contains the target.”

Repair target: identify the stated/proved structure and the smallest local chain first.

### Error 6 — synthetic loyalty

Claim: “Because the problem is about a circumcircle, coordinates are not allowed.”

Repair target: compare representation cost after preserving the circle condition.

## C. ADOPT — first move only

Do not solve fully. Write the first two useful lines.

1. A cyclic quadrilateral has one angle `117°`.
2. A convex quadrilateral has opposite angles `83°` and `97°`.
3. A tangent at `T` is drawn to a circle with centre `O`, and a chord through `T` is also present.
4. From external point `P`, two tangents touch the same circle.
5. Chords `AB` and `CD` intersect at interior point `P`.
6. Tangent `PT` and secant `PAB` come from the same external point.
7. Two circles intersect at `A,B`; their centres are known.
8. A symmetric isosceles triangle has a circumcircle and a chord parallel to the base through a point on the symmetry axis.

## D. TRANSFER — changed surface

1. A quadrilateral problem never mentions a circle, but an angle chase gives one pair of opposite angles summing to `180°`. Explain the representation upgrade and what new relations become legal.
2. A road touches a circular park at `T`, and two measured paths from an external point meet the park at different places. Translate the useful tangent/secant information into one power statement.
3. Two sensors are centres of overlapping circular ranges. Their two boundary intersections are `A,B`. Explain the direction information supplied by the common chord before any coordinates are introduced.
4. A rectangular coordinate configuration contains four concyclic points and two equal-length constraints. Explain why cyclicity is not decorative information and where it should enter the algebra.
5. A diagram contains a tangent and many ordinary triangle angles. Explain how you decide whether alternate segment is cheaper than a generic angle chase.
6. Construct a problem where a circle is present but the cheapest solution is coordinate geometry. State the circle relation that must still be preserved explicitly.

## E. Six-question assimilation test

Choose one problem from Section D and answer all six questions before completing its solution.

1. **What did you notice?** State the circle structure that matters.
2. **Why is that structure legal?** State the hypothesis or proof that earns it.
3. **What clue would make you think of it again?** Give a future recognition cue.
4. **What similar-looking situation needs a different method?** State one contrast pair.
5. **Can you write the first two useful lines without help?** Write them.
6. **Can you solve a disguised version?** Change the surface while preserving the same circle invariant and outline the route.

## Readiness rule

You are not ready merely because you can recite circle theorems. You are ready when you can refuse an unjustified cyclic/tangent claim, identify the chord or power point that owns the relation, choose a short synthetic chain when one exists, switch to coordinates when the metric structure makes them cheaper, and start a disguised circle problem without a chapter label.
