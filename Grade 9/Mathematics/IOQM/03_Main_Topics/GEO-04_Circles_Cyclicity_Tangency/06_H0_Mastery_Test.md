# Mixed Mastery Test

Solve each problem without method labels or hints. Use only circle relations whose hypotheses are stated or proved.

1. In a circle with centre `O`, chord `AB` subtends central angle `146°`. Point `C` lies on the corresponding arc-side so that `∠ACB` subtends the same chord. Determine `∠ACB`.

2. `ABCD` is cyclic and `∠B=68°`. Determine `∠D`.

3. A convex quadrilateral has `∠A=95°` and `∠C=85°`. State the strongest circle-structure conclusion justified by this information.

4. From external point `P`, tangent `PT` and secant `PAB` meet the same circle. If `PT=15` and `PA=9`, determine `PB`.

5. Chords `AB` and `CD` intersect at `P` inside a circle. If `PA=6`, `PB=8` and `PC=4`, determine `PD`.

6. Two secants from external point `P` meet a circle in order `P-A-B` and `P-C-D`. If `PA=3`, `PB=18` and `PC=6`, determine `PD`.

7. A tangent at `A` forms an angle of `41°` with chord `AB`. Point `C` lies on the opposite arc. Determine `∠ACB`.

8. From an external point `P`, tangents touch a circle at `A,B`. Suppose `PA=17` and `∠APB=60°`. Determine `AB`.

9. Two circles with distinct centres `O1,O2` intersect at `A,B`. Prove that `O1O2` is perpendicular to the common chord `AB`.

10. A circle has equation `x^2+y^2=25`. A horizontal chord lies on the line `y=3`. Determine the chord length, and explain why coordinates are a natural route here.

11. A sketch shows line `l` touching a circle at `T`, but the statement does not say `l` is tangent and gives no right-angle information. A proposed solution writes `OT ⟂ l`, where `O` is the centre. Explain precisely why this move is invalid and what would be sufficient to justify it.

12. A tangent at `A` and chord `AB` form an angle of `52°`. Points `B,C,D,A` lie on one circle, with `C` in the opposite segment from the tangent angle. Determine `∠ACB`, then state one additional cyclic relation involving an angle at `D` that also subtends chord `AB` from the same segment as `C`.
