## Q36. Repeated roots and Vieta coefficients

### Question

For distinct complex numbers

\[
z_1,z_2,\ldots,z_{673},
\]

the polynomial

\[
(x-z_1)^3(x-z_2)^3\cdots(x-z_{673})^3
\]

can be written as

\[
x^{2019}+20x^{2018}+19x^{2017}+g(x),
\]

where \(\deg g\le2016\).

If

\[
\left|
\sum_{1\le j<k\le673}z_jz_k
\right|
=
\frac mn
\]

for relatively prime positive integers \(m,n\), find \(m+n\).

**Answer:** \(\boxed{352}\)

**Original source identified:** 2019 AIME I, Problem 10.

> **Notation correction:** The ALLEN text extraction drops the absolute-value bars. They are restored from the published AIME statement.

### Short solution

Let

\[
S=\sum_{i=1}^{673}z_i,
\qquad
E=\sum_{j<k}z_jz_k.
\]

Each \(z_i\) occurs as a root three times. By Vieta,

\[
3S=-20,
\]

so

\[
S=-\frac{20}{3}.
\]

The second elementary symmetric sum of the \(2019\) roots is \(19\).

It consists of:

- choosing two copies of the same \(z_i\): contribution \(3\sum z_i^2\);
- choosing copies of two distinct \(z_i,z_j\): contribution \(9E\).

Thus

\[
19=3\sum z_i^2+9E.
\]

Also

\[
S^2=\sum z_i^2+2E,
\]

so

\[
\sum z_i^2=\frac{400}{9}-2E.
\]

Therefore

\[
19
=
3\left(\frac{400}{9}-2E\right)+9E
=
\frac{400}{3}+3E.
\]

Hence

\[
E=-\frac{343}{9}.
\]

Thus

\[
|E|=\frac{343}{9},
\]

and

\[
m+n=343+9=\boxed{352}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

With repeated roots, count multiplicities carefully in Vieta sums.

If every root occurs \(r\) times:

- the first symmetric sum gets a factor \(r\);
- a same-root pair contributes \(\binom r2 z_i^2\);
- a distinct-root pair contributes \(r^2z_iz_j\).

## Q37. Arithmetic progression meets geometric progression

### Question

In an increasing sequence of four positive integers, the first three terms form an arithmetic progression, the last three form a geometric progression, and the first and fourth terms differ by \(30\).

Find the sum of the four terms.

**Answer:** \(\boxed{129}\)

**Original source identified:** 2003 AIME I, Problem 8.

### Short solution

Write the first three terms as

\[
a-d,\quad a,\quad a+d,
\]

where \(d>0\).

Since the last three are in geometric progression,

\[
\frac{a+d}{a}
=
\frac{T_4}{a+d},
\]

so

\[
T_4=\frac{(a+d)^2}{a}.
\]

The first and fourth differ by \(30\):

\[
\frac{(a+d)^2}{a}-(a-d)=30.
\]

Simplifying,

\[
3d+\frac{d^2}{a}=30,
\]

so

\[
a=\frac{d^2}{3(10-d)}.
\]

Thus \(d<10\). Since \(a,d\) are integers, the denominator's factor \(3\) forces \(3\mid d\), so

\[
d\in\{3,6,9\}.
\]

- \(d=3\) does not give integer \(a\);
- \(d=6\) gives \(a=3\), making \(a-d<0\);
- \(d=9\) gives \(a=27\).

Hence the sequence is

\[
18,\ 27,\ 36,\ 48,
\]

whose sum is

\[
\boxed{129}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

When three consecutive terms form an AP, parameterize them symmetrically:

\[
a-d,\ a,\ a+d.
\]

Then the GP condition on the last three is simply

\[
a\cdot T_4=(a+d)^2.
\]

Integrality and positivity can drastically shrink the remaining cases.

## Q38. Integer-score AP/GP basketball game

### Question

A high school basketball game between the Raiders and Wildcats was tied at the end of the first quarter.

- The Raiders' four quarterly scores form an increasing geometric sequence.
- The Wildcats' four quarterly scores form an increasing arithmetic sequence.
- The Raiders win by exactly \(1\) point.
- Neither team scores \(100\) or more points in total.

What is the total number of points scored by both teams in the first half?

**Answer:** \(\boxed{34}\)

**Original source identified:** 2010 AMC 10B, Problem 24; also 2010 AMC 12B, Problem 19.

### Short solution

Let the common first-quarter score be \(A\).

Write the Raiders' ratio as

\[
r=\frac pq>1
\]

in lowest terms. Since all four Raider scores are integers,

\[
q^3\mid A.
\]

If \(q\ge3\), even the smallest possible case \(p=q+1\), \(A=q^3\) gives total Raider points exceeding \(100\). Hence \(q\le2\).

### Case \(q=2\)

The smallest possible \(p\) is \(3\), giving Raider scores

\[
8,12,18,27
\]

up to a positive integer multiplier. The total is already \(65\), so the multiplier is \(1\). The Wildcats would need total \(64\), but

\[
4(8)+6d=64
\]

has no integral \(d\). So this case is impossible.

### Case \(q=1\)

Thus \(r\) is an integer.

For \(r=2\), the Raiders total

\[
15A<100,
\]

so \(1\le A\le6\).

If the Wildcats' common difference is \(d\), their total is

\[
4A+6d.
\]

Since Raiders win by \(1\),

\[
15A=(4A+6d)+1,
\]

so

\[
6d=11A-1.
\]

This forces

\[
A\equiv5\pmod6,
\]

hence \(A=5\), and then

\[
d=9.
\]

Ratios \(r=3,4\) fail the corresponding divisibility condition, and \(r\ge5\) already exceeds the score bound.

Therefore first-half scores are:

- Raiders: \(5+10=15\);
- Wildcats: \(5+14=19\).

Total:

\[
15+19=\boxed{34}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

If an integer geometric progression has rational ratio \(p/q\) in lowest terms, integrality of several consecutive terms forces large powers of \(q\) into the first term. A global bound can then eliminate nonintegral ratios quickly.

## Q39. Missing consecutive sheets

### Question

Hiram's algebra notes are \(50\) pages long, printed on \(25\) sheets:

- sheet \(1\) contains pages \(1,2\);
- sheet \(2\) contains pages \(3,4\);
- and so on.

A roommate removes a consecutive set of sheets from the middle. The average page number on all remaining pages is exactly \(19\).

How many sheets were removed?

**Answer:** \(\boxed{13}\)

**Original source identified:** 2021 AMC 10A, Problem 22.

### Short solution

The sum of all page numbers is

\[
1+2+\cdots+50=1275.
\]

Suppose \(k\) consecutive sheets are removed, starting at sheet \(a\).

Then \(50-2k\) pages remain, with total

\[
19(50-2k)=950-38k.
\]

So the removed pages sum to

\[
1275-(950-38k)=325+38k.
\]

The removed pages are consecutive, from

\[
2a-1
\]

through

\[
2a+2k-2.
\]

Their sum is

\[
k(4a+2k-3).
\]

Thus

\[
k(4a+2k-3)=325+38k,
\]

or

\[
k(4a+2k-41)=325.
\]

Hence \(k\mid325\). With \(1\le k\le25\),

\[
k\in\{1,5,13,25\}.
\]

Checking:

- \(k=1\): starting sheet would be outside the notes;
- \(k=5\): the block would run past sheet \(25\);
- \(k=25\): removes all sheets;
- \(k=13\): gives \(a=10\), a valid middle block.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{13}
\]

sheets were removed.

### IOQM tip / trick

When consecutive pages/sheets are removed, express their sum by

\[
\text{number of terms}\times\text{average of endpoints}.
\]

Then use divisibility before doing location casework.

## Q40. Global min and max on a simplex

### Question

Let \(x,y,z\) be nonnegative real numbers satisfying

\[
x+y+z=2.
\]

Let \(m\) and \(n\) be the minimum and maximum possible values, respectively, of

\[
F=xy+yz+xz-xyz.
\]

Find \(m+n\).

**Answer:** \(\boxed{\frac{28}{27}}\)

### Short solution

First,

\[
F=xy+z(x+y-xy).
\]

Because \(x,y\ge0\) and \(x+y\le2\),

\[
xy\le\frac{(x+y)^2}{4}\le x+y,
\]

so \(F\ge0\).

Equality occurs at

\[
(x,y,z)=(2,0,0),
\]

so

\[
m=0.
\]

For the maximum, fix \(z\) and put

\[
x+y=2-z,\qquad t=xy.
\]

Then

\[
F=z(2-z)+(1-z)t.
\]

If \(z\ge1\), the coefficient \(1-z\le0\), so

\[
F\le z(2-z)\le1.
\]

If \(0\le z\le1\), then \(1-z\ge0\), so \(F\) is maximized when \(xy\) is maximized, i.e. when

\[
x=y=\frac{2-z}{2}.
\]

Write \(x=y=u\), so \(z=2-2u\) and \(\frac12\le u\le1\). Then

\[
F(u)=4u-5u^2+2u^3.
\]

Differentiate:

\[
F'(u)=2(3u-2)(u-1).
\]

Thus the interior maximum occurs at

\[
u=\frac23,
\]

giving

\[
x=y=z=\frac23
\]

and

\[
n=3\cdot\frac49-\frac8{27}
=\frac{28}{27}.
\]

Therefore

\[
m+n=\boxed{\frac{28}{27}}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

For symmetric optimization with a fixed sum, **smoothing** is often stronger than guessing equality.

Fix one variable. If the expression is linear in \(xy\), then the sign of its coefficient tells you whether \(xy\) should be maximized (forcing \(x=y\)) or minimized (forcing a boundary case).
