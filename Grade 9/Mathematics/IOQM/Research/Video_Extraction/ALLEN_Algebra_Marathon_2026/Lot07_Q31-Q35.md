## Q31. Integer-coefficient polynomial at radicals

### Question

Suppose \(P(x)\) is a cubic polynomial with integer coefficients such that

\[
P(\sqrt5)=5
\]

and

\[
P(\sqrt[3]{5})=5\sqrt[3]{5}.
\]

Compute \(P(5)\).

**Answer:** \(\boxed{-95}\)

**Original source identified:** HMMT February 2023, Algebra & Number Theory, Problem 1.

### Short solution

Write

\[
P(x)=ax^3+bx^2+cx+d,
\qquad a,b,c,d\in\mathbb Z.
\]

From \(P(\sqrt5)=5\),

\[
(5a+c)\sqrt5+(5b+d)=5.
\]

Since \(\sqrt5\notin\mathbb Q\),

\[
5a+c=0,\qquad 5b+d=5.
\]

Let \(u=\sqrt[3]5\). Because \(1,u,u^2\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\),

\[
P(u)=5a+bu^2+cu+d=5u
\]

implies

\[
b=0,\qquad c=5,\qquad 5a+d=0.
\]

Hence

\[
a=-1,\quad b=0,\quad c=5,\quad d=5,
\]

so

\[
P(x)=-x^3+5x+5.
\]

Thus

\[
P(5)=-125+25+5=\boxed{-95}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

If an integer/rational-coefficient polynomial is evaluated at an algebraic irrational, compare coefficients in a basis of its number field:

\[
1,\sqrt5
\]

or

\[
1,\sqrt[3]5,\sqrt[3]{25}.
\]

Linear independence often determines the polynomial immediately.

## Q32. A self-referential polynomial identity

### Question

Suppose \(P(x)\) has real coefficients and

\[
P(t)=P(1)t^2+P(P(1))t+P(P(P(1)))
\]

for every real \(t\).

Compute the largest possible value of

\[
P(P(P(P(1)))).
\]

**Answer:** \(\boxed{\frac19}\)

**Original source identified:** HMMT February 2023, Algebra & Number Theory, Problem 4.

### Short solution

Set

\[
A=P(1),\qquad B=P(A),\qquad C=P(B).
\]

Then

\[
P(t)=At^2+Bt+C.
\]

Putting \(t=1\),

\[
A=A+B+C,
\]

so

\[
C=-B.
\]

Next,

\[
B=P(A)=A^3+AB-B,
\]

hence

\[
B(2-A)=A^3.
\]

Also

\[
C=P(B)=AB^2+B^2+C.
\]

Since \(C=-B\),

\[
(A+1)B^2=0.
\]

So either:

1. \(B=0\). Then \(A=0\), \(C=0\), and \(P\equiv0\), giving the target \(0\).

2. \(A=-1\). Then

\[
B=\frac{A^3}{2-A}=-\frac13,
\qquad
C=\frac13.
\]

Thus

\[
P(t)=-t^2-\frac13t+\frac13.
\]

The target is \(P(C)=P(\frac13)\):

\[
-\frac19-\frac19+\frac13=\frac19.
\]

Therefore the largest possible value is

\[
\boxed{\frac19}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

For nested expressions such as

\[
P(1),\ P(P(1)),\ P(P(P(1))),
\]

name them. The functional identity itself often forces \(P\) to have very low degree and gives algebraic consistency equations among those named values.

**Problem and answer:** Fully verified.

## Q33. Convert a nonlinear system to an equation for \(xyz\)

### Question

Real numbers \(x,y,z\) satisfy

\[
x+xy+xyz=1,
\]

\[
y+yz+xyz=2,
\]

\[
z+xz+xyz=4.
\]

The largest possible value of \(xyz\) is

\[
\frac{a+b\sqrt c}{d},
\]

where \(a,b,c,d\) are integers, \(d>0\), \(c\) is square-free, and

\[
\gcd(a,b,d)=1.
\]

Find

\[
1000a+100b+10c+d.
\]

**Answer:** \(\boxed{5272}\)

**Original source identified:** HMMT November 2021, General Round, Problem 10.

### Short solution

Let

\[
p=xyz.
\]

Multiplying the three given equations after isolating the factored forms gives

\[
p(8-2p)=(1-p)(2-p)(4-p).
\]

Equivalently,

\[
p^3-9p^2+22p-8=0.
\]

Factor:

\[
(p-4)(p^2-5p+2)=0.
\]

Thus

\[
p=4,\qquad
p=\frac{5\pm\sqrt{17}}2.
\]

The largest is

\[
\frac{5+\sqrt{17}}2.
\]

Hence

\[
a=5,\quad b=1,\quad c=17,\quad d=2,
\]

and

\[
1000a+100b+10c+d
=
5000+100+170+2
=
\boxed{5272}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

If a nonlinear cyclic system repeatedly contains \(xyz\), introduce

\[
p=xyz.
\]

Look for products of the equations or shifted factorizations that eliminate \(x,y,z\) and leave a single polynomial in \(p\).

**Problem and answer:** Fully verified against the published HMMT problem/solution.  
**Video numbering:** **Level D.**

## Q34. Reciprocal sums over roots without solving the cubic

### Question

Let

\[
f(x)=x^3+3x-1
\]

have roots \(a,b,c\). Suppose

\[
\frac1{a^3+b^3}
+
\frac1{b^3+c^3}
+
\frac1{c^3+a^3}
=
\frac mn,
\]

where \(m,n\) are relatively prime positive integers.

Find

\[
100m+n.
\]

**Answer:** \(\boxed{3989}\)

### Short solution

Vieta gives

\[
a+b+c=0,\qquad abc=1.
\]

Since \(a+b=-c\),

\[
a^3+b^3
=
(a+b)^3-3ab(a+b)
=
-c^3+3abc
=
3-c^3.
\]

But \(c^3+3c-1=0\), so

\[
c^3=1-3c
\]

and therefore

\[
a^3+b^3=2+3c.
\]

Cyclically,

\[
S=
\sum_{r\in\{a,b,c\}}\frac1{2+3r}.
\]

Let \(p(x)=x^3+3x-1\). Then

\[
S
=
\frac13\sum_r\frac1{r+\frac23}
=
-\frac13\frac{p'(-2/3)}{p(-2/3)}.
\]

Now

\[
p'(-2/3)=\frac{13}{3},
\qquad
p(-2/3)=-\frac{89}{27}.
\]

Thus

\[
S=\frac{39}{89}.
\]

Hence

\[
100m+n=3900+89=\boxed{3989}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

Memorize the logarithmic derivative identity

\[
\frac{p'(t)}{p(t)}
=
\sum_i\frac1{t-r_i}.
\]

It turns sums of reciprocals involving all roots into one substitution into \(p\) and \(p'\).

## Q35. Reciprocal shift identities

### Question

Let \(a,b,c,x,y,z\) be complex numbers such that

\[
a=\frac{b+c}{x-2},\qquad
b=\frac{c+a}{y-2},\qquad
c=\frac{a+b}{z-2}.
\]

If

\[
xy+yz+zx=67
\]

and

\[
x+y+z=2010,
\]

find \(xyz\).

**Answer:** \(\boxed{-5892}\)

**Original source identified:** HMMT February 2010, Algebra Test, Problem 7.

### Short solution

Let

\[
S=a+b+c.
\]

From

\[
a(x-2)=b+c=S-a
\]

we get

\[
a(x-1)=S.
\]

Similarly,

\[
b(y-1)=S,\qquad c(z-1)=S.
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac1{x-1}+\frac1{y-1}+\frac1{z-1}
=
\frac{a+b+c}{S}
=1.
\]

Clearing denominators,

\[
(y-1)(z-1)+(z-1)(x-1)+(x-1)(y-1)
=
(x-1)(y-1)(z-1).
\]

Let

\[
s=x+y+z,\qquad q=xy+yz+zx,\qquad r=xyz.
\]

Then

\[
q-2s+3=r-q+s-1.
\]

Hence

\[
r=2q-3s+4.
\]

Substitute \(q=67\) and \(s=2010\):

\[
r=134-6030+4=\boxed{-5892}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

Relations like

\[
a=\frac{b+c}{x-2}
\]

may become much cleaner after introducing the total \(S=a+b+c\). Here they turn into

\[
a(x-1)=S,
\]

which produces a reciprocal identity in \(x-1,y-1,z-1\). Then use elementary symmetric sums.
