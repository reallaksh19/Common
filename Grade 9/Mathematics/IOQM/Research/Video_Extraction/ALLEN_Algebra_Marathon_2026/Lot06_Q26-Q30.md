## Q26. Shifted cubic roots and Newton sums

### Question

The equation

\[
(x-\sqrt[3]{13})(x-\sqrt[3]{53})(x-\sqrt[3]{103})=\frac13
\]

has three distinct real solutions \(r,s,t\). Calculate

\[
r^3+s^3+t^3.
\]

**Answer:** \(\boxed{170}\)

### Short solution

Let

\[
\alpha=\sqrt[3]{13},\quad \beta=\sqrt[3]{53},\quad \gamma=\sqrt[3]{103}.
\]

The equation is

\[
(x-\alpha)(x-\beta)(x-\gamma)-\frac13=0.
\]

Compared with the cubic whose roots are \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\), the coefficients of \(x^2\) and \(x\) are unchanged. Thus

\[
r+s+t=\alpha+\beta+\gamma
\]

and

\[
rs+st+tr=\alpha\beta+\beta\gamma+\gamma\alpha.
\]

Only the product changes:

\[
rst=\alpha\beta\gamma+\frac13.
\]

Using

\[
r^3+s^3+t^3
=(r+s+t)^3-3(r+s+t)(rs+st+tr)+3rst,
\]

the first two symmetric parts agree with those for \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\), so

\[
r^3+s^3+t^3
=
\alpha^3+\beta^3+\gamma^3+3\left(\frac13\right).
\]

Hence

\[
13+53+103+1=\boxed{170}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

If a monic polynomial is changed **only in its constant term**, all elementary symmetric sums except the product of the roots remain unchanged. Newton-sum identities can then compare power sums without solving for any root.

## Q27. Cyclic system — all ordered triples

### Question

Determine all triples of real numbers \((x,y,z)\) such that

\[
xyz=8,
\]

\[
x^2y+y^2z+z^2x=73,
\]

and

\[
x(y-z)^2+y(z-x)^2+z(x-y)^2=98.
\]

**Answer:**

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&(1,1,8),(1,8,1),(8,1,1),\\
&(4,4,\tfrac12),(4,\tfrac12,4),(\tfrac12,4,4)
\end{aligned}}
\]

**Original source identified:** 2005 Iberoamerican Mathematical Olympiad (OIM), Problem 1.

> **Answer-key correction:** The ALLEN key lists only \((1,1,8)\) and \((4,4,\tfrac12)\). For ordered triples, that is incomplete. The original problem has all six triples above.

### Short solution

Expand the third condition:

\[
x(y-z)^2+y(z-x)^2+z(x-y)^2
\]

\[
=
(x^2y+y^2z+z^2x)
+(xy^2+yz^2+zx^2)-6xyz.
\]

Using the first two equations,

\[
98=73+(xy^2+yz^2+zx^2)-48,
\]

so

\[
xy^2+yz^2+zx^2=73.
\]

Therefore

\[
x^2y+y^2z+z^2x
=
xy^2+yz^2+zx^2.
\]

Their difference factors as

\[
(x-y)(y-z)(z-x)=0.
\]

Thus at least two variables are equal.

Suppose \(y=z=u\) and \(x=v\). Then

\[
vu^2=8
\]

and

\[
v^2u+u^3+u^2v=73.
\]

Since \(u^2v=8\), multiplying the remaining relation by \(u^3\) gives

\[
u^6-65u^3+64=0,
\]

so

\[
(u^3-1)(u^3-64)=0.
\]

Hence \(u=1\) or \(u=4\), giving

\[
(v,u,u)=(8,1,1)
\quad\text{or}\quad
\left(\frac12,4,4\right).
\]

The corresponding permutations all satisfy the original system, giving six ordered triples.

### IOQM tip / trick

For cyclic cubic sums, compare the two orientations

\[
x^2y+y^2z+z^2x
\quad\text{and}\quad
xy^2+yz^2+zx^2.
\]

Their difference factors as a Vandermonde product:

\[
x^2y+y^2z+z^2x-(xy^2+yz^2+zx^2)
=-(x-y)(y-z)(z-x).
\]

Equality therefore forces two variables to coincide.

**Answer:** Fully verified; six ordered triples.

## Q28. Rational equation with symmetric poles

### Question

Let \(m\) be the largest real solution of

\[
\frac3{x-3}+\frac5{x-5}+\frac{17}{x-17}+\frac{19}{x-19}
=
x^2-11x-4.
\]

There are positive integers \(a,b,c\) such that

\[
m=a+\sqrt{\,b+\sqrt c\,}.
\]

Find \(a+b+c\).

**Answer:** \(\boxed{263}\)

**Original source identified:** 2014 AIME I, Problem 14.

### Short solution

Add \(4\) to both sides. Since

\[
\frac{k}{x-k}+1=\frac{x}{x-k},
\]

we get

\[
x\left(
\frac1{x-3}+\frac1{x-5}
+\frac1{x-17}+\frac1{x-19}
\right)=x(x-11).
\]

Besides \(x=0\), set

\[
t=x-11.
\]

Pair the symmetric denominators:

\[
\frac1{t+8}+\frac1{t-8}
+
\frac1{t+6}+\frac1{t-6}
=t.
\]

Thus

\[
\frac{2t}{t^2-64}+\frac{2t}{t^2-36}=t.
\]

The case \(t=0\) gives \(x=11\). Otherwise divide by \(t\) and put \(u=t^2\):

\[
\frac2{u-64}+\frac2{u-36}=1.
\]

This gives

\[
u^2-104u+2504=0,
\]

so

\[
u=52\pm\sqrt{200}.
\]

The largest real solution is therefore

\[
m=11+\sqrt{52+\sqrt{200}}.
\]

Hence

\[
a+b+c=11+52+200=\boxed{263}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

When numerators match pole locations, use

\[
\frac{k}{x-k}+1=\frac{x}{x-k}.
\]

Also look for denominators symmetric around a midpoint. Shifting \(x\) to that midpoint often converts the equation into one in \(t^2\).

**Problem and answer:** Fully verified from the published AIME source.  
**All candidate branches:** Checked; the displayed one is the largest.

x^3+2x^2+3x-4=0
\]

with distinct roots \(a,b,c\), and asks for

\[
\frac1{a(b^2+c^2-a^2)}
+\frac1{b(c^2+a^2-b^2)}
+\frac1{c(a^2+b^2-c^2)}.

### Short verification of the literal extraction

For

\[
p(x)=x^3+2x^2+3x-4,
\]

Vieta gives

\[
a+b+c=-2,\qquad abc=4.
\]

For a root \(a\),

\[
a(b^2+c^2-a^2)
=
4(a-1)(a+2).
\]

Hence the requested literal-expression value is

\[
\frac14\sum_{p(a)=0}\frac1{(a-1)(a+2)}.
\]

Using

\[
\frac1{(a-1)(a+2)}
=
\frac13\left(\frac1{a-1}-\frac1{a+2}\right)
\]

and the logarithmic-derivative identity

\[
\sum_i\frac1{t-a_i}=\frac{p'(t)}{p(t)},
\]

we obtain

\[
S=
\frac1{12}
\left(
-\frac{p'(1)}{p(1)}
+
\frac{p'(-2)}{p(-2)}
\right).
\]

Now

\[
p(1)=2,\quad p'(1)=10,\quad
p(-2)=-10,\quad p'(-2)=7.
\]

Therefore

\[
S
=
\frac1{12}
\left(-5-\frac7{10}\right)
=
-\frac{19}{40},
\]

not \(\frac18\).

### IOQM tip / trick

For a monic polynomial \(p(x)=\prod_i(x-r_i)\),

\[
\frac{p'(t)}{p(t)}
=
\sum_i\frac1{t-r_i}.
\]

This is a powerful way to evaluate reciprocal expressions over all roots without solving the polynomial.

It is also a useful **verification tool**: if a recovered statement and answer key disagree, check the literal mathematics before “repairing” any sign.

## Q30. Three integers from sum and sum of squares

### Question

Suppose integers

\[
a<b<c
\]

satisfy

\[
a+b+c=95
\]

and

\[
a^2+b^2+c^2=3083.
\]

Find \(c\).

**Answer:** \(\boxed{37}\)

### Short solution

Use

\[
(a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2
=
3(a^2+b^2+c^2)-(a+b+c)^2.
\]

Thus

\[
(a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2
=
3(3083)-95^2
=
224.
\]

Let

\[
p=b-a>0,\qquad q=c-b>0.
\]

Then \(c-a=p+q\), so

\[
p^2+q^2+(p+q)^2=224,
\]

or

\[
p^2+pq+q^2=112.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
(2p+q)^2+3q^2=448.
\]

Since \(q\le10\), checking the possible positive integers \(q\) for which \(448-3q^2\) is a square gives

\[
(p,q)=(8,4)\quad\text{or}\quad(4,8).
\]

Now

\[
3a+2p+q=95.
\]

For \((p,q)=(4,8)\), this does not give integral \(a\). For \((8,4)\),

\[
a=\frac{95-16-4}{3}=25.
\]

Hence

\[
b=33,\qquad c=37.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{c=37}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

When both

\[
a+b+c
\quad\text{and}\quad
a^2+b^2+c^2
\]

are known, the identity

\[
(a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2
=
3\sum a^2-(\sum a)^2
\]

often turns an unordered-looking problem into a small Diophantine equation in consecutive gaps.
