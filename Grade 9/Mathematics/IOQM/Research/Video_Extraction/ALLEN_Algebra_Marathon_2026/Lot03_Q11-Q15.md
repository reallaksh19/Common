## Worksheet candidate 11 — Cubic with exactly two real roots

### Question

Find the unique real number \(c\) such that the polynomial

\[
x^3+cx+c
\]

has exactly two real roots.

**Answer:**

\[
\boxed{-\frac{27}{4}}.
\]

**Original source identified:** 2020 Carnegie Mellon Informatics and Mathematics Competition (CMIMC), Algebra & Number Theory, Problem 2.

### Short solution

A real cubic with exactly two **distinct** real roots must have one double root and one simple real root.

Let the double root be \(r\). Then \(r\) is a common root of

\[
f(x)=x^3+cx+c
\]

and

\[
f'(x)=3x^2+c.
\]

Thus

\[
r^3+cr+c=0
\]

and

\[
3r^2+c=0,
\]

so

\[
c=-3r^2.
\]

Substitute into \(f(r)=0\):

\[
r^3-3r^3-3r^2=0,
\]

hence

\[
-r^2(2r+3)=0.
\]

Therefore

\[
r=0
\quad\text{or}\quad
r=-\frac32.
\]

If \(r=0\), then \(c=0\) and \(f(x)=x^3\), which has only one distinct real root.

Hence

\[
r=-\frac32,
\]

and

\[
c=-3\left(\frac94\right)
=-\frac{27}{4}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{c=-\frac{27}{4}}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

“Exactly two real roots” for a cubic with real coefficients usually signals a **repeated root**.

Instead of using the full cubic discriminant, impose

\[
f(r)=0,\qquad f'(r)=0.
\]

A repeated root is precisely a common root of a polynomial and its derivative.

## Worksheet candidate 12 — Symmetric sums and reciprocals

### Question

Suppose \(x,y,z\) are nonzero complex numbers such that

\[
(x+y+z)(x^2+y^2+z^2)=x^3+y^3+z^3.
\]

Compute

\[
(x+y+z)\left(\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z\right).
\]

**Answer:** \(\boxed{3}\)

**Original source identified:** A 2017 Carnegie Mellon / CMIMC problem; the exact round/problem number has not been independently pinned down from the currently accessible archive.

### Short solution

Let

\[
s=x+y+z,\qquad
q=xy+yz+zx,\qquad
r=xyz.
\]

Then

\[
x^2+y^2+z^2=s^2-2q
\]

and

\[
x^3+y^3+z^3=s^3-3sq+3r.
\]

The given equation becomes

\[
s(s^2-2q)=s^3-3sq+3r.
\]

Cancel \(s^3\):

\[
-2sq=-3sq+3r,
\]

so

\[
sq=3r.
\]

Because \(xyz=r\ne0\),

\[
\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z=\frac{q}{r}.
\]

Therefore

\[
(x+y+z)\left(\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z\right)
=s\frac qr
=\frac{sq}{r}
=\boxed3.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

For symmetric expressions in three variables, introduce the elementary symmetric sums

\[
s=x+y+z,\qquad q=xy+yz+zx,\qquad r=xyz.
\]

Memorize

\[
x^2+y^2+z^2=s^2-2q
\]

and

\[
x^3+y^3+z^3=s^3-3sq+3r.
\]

Reciprocal sums then become

\[
\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z=\frac{q}{r}.
\]

**Problem and answer:** Fully verified.  
**Original-source identification:** Strong identification to a 2017 Carnegie Mellon problem, but exact round numbering remains unconfirmed.

## Worksheet candidate 13 — Reciprocal-ratio substitution

### Question

Let \(x\) and \(y\) be positive real numbers. Define

\[
a=1+\frac{x}{y},
\qquad
b=1+\frac{y}{x}.
\]

If

\[
a^2+b^2=15,
\]

compute

\[
a^3+b^3.
\]

**Answer:** \(\boxed{50}\)

**Original source identified:** HMMT February 2019, Algebra Round, Problem 3.

### Short solution

Since

\[
a-1=\frac{x}{y},
\qquad
b-1=\frac{y}{x},
\]

we have

\[
(a-1)(b-1)=1.
\]

Thus

\[
ab-a-b+1=1,
\]

so

\[
ab=a+b.
\]

Let

\[
t=a+b=ab.
\]

Then

\[
a^2+b^2=(a+b)^2-2ab=t^2-2t.
\]

Hence

\[
t^2-2t=15,
\]

so

\[
(t-5)(t+3)=0.
\]

Because \(a,b>1\), we have \(t>0\), hence

\[
t=5.
\]

Finally,

\[
a^3+b^3=(a+b)^3-3ab(a+b)
=t^3-3t^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
a^3+b^3=125-75=\boxed{50}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

If a problem contains both

\[
\frac{x}{y}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{y}{x},
\]

look for a reciprocal relation.

Here

\[
(a-1)(b-1)=1
\]

immediately yields the powerful simplification

\[
ab=a+b.
\]

This can collapse all higher symmetric powers of \(a,b\) to a single parameter.

**Problem and answer:** Fully verified.  
**Lecture numbering:** **Level D — not video-sequence confirmed.**

## Worksheet candidate 14 — Odd powers forced by a symmetric condition

### Question

Let \(m,n,p\) be real numbers for which all displayed reciprocals are defined and

\[
(m+n+p)
\left(
\frac1m+\frac1n+\frac1p
\right)=1.
\]

Find all possible values of

\[
\frac1{(m+n+p)^{2023}}
-\frac1{m^{2023}}
-\frac1{n^{2023}}
-\frac1{p^{2023}}.
\]

**Answer:** \(\boxed{0}\)

**Original source identified:** 2023 Bangladesh Mathematical Olympiad National Round, Secondary, Problem 5.

### Short solution

Set

\[
s=m+n+p,\qquad
q=mn+np+pm,\qquad
r=mnp.
\]

The condition is

\[
s\frac qr=1,
\]

hence

\[
sq=r.
\]

The monic cubic with roots \(m,n,p\) is

\[
t^3-st^2+qt-r.
\]

Since \(r=sq\),

\[
t^3-st^2+qt-sq
=(t-s)(t^2+q).
\]

Therefore one of \(m,n,p\) equals \(s\), while the other two are roots of

\[
t^2+q=0.
\]

Because \(m,n,p\) are real, those remaining roots are opposites. Thus, after relabeling,

\[
(m,n,p)=(s,u,-u)
\]

for some nonzero real \(u\).

Since \(2023\) is odd,

\[
\frac1{u^{2023}}
+
\frac1{(-u)^{2023}}
=0.
\]

Hence

\[
\frac1{s^{2023}}
-\frac1{s^{2023}}
-\frac1{u^{2023}}
-\frac1{(-u)^{2023}}
=0.
\]

Therefore the only possible value is

\[
\boxed0.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

A symmetric condition involving

\[
m+n+p
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac1m+\frac1n+\frac1p
\]

should be rewritten using elementary symmetric sums:

\[
\frac1m+\frac1n+\frac1p
=\frac{mn+np+pm}{mnp}.
\]

If this produces

\[
r=sq,
\]

test the cubic whose roots are the variables:

\[
t^3-st^2+qt-r.
\]

Here it factors beautifully as

\[
(t-s)(t^2+q),
\]

revealing an opposite pair. Odd powers then cancel.

**Problem and answer:** Fully verified.  
**Flattened worksheet notation:** Restored from the published Bangladesh Mathematical Olympiad statement.

## Worksheet candidate 15 — Counting cubic polynomials by root multiplicity

### Question

Find the number of cubic polynomials

\[
p(x)=x^3+ax^2+bx+c,
\]

where \(a,b,c\) are integers in

\[
\{-20,-19,-18,\ldots,18,19,20\},
\]

such that there is a unique integer \(m\ne2\) with

\[
p(m)=p(2).
\]

**Answer:** \(\boxed{738}\)

**Original source identified:** 2023 AIME I, Problem 9.

### Short solution

Since

\[
p(m)-p(2)
\]

is divisible by \(m-2\),

\[
p(m)-p(2)
=(m-2)
\left[
m^2+(a+2)m+(2a+b+4)
\right].
\]

Thus for \(m\ne2\), the desired integers are precisely the integer roots of

\[
q(m)=m^2+(a+2)m+(2a+b+4).
\]

Because \(q\) is monic with integer coefficients, if it has one integer root then its other root is also an integer.

For there to be exactly one allowed integer \(m\ne2\), there are two possibilities.

### Case 1: \(q\) has a double integer root \(m\ne2\)

The discriminant is zero:

\[
(a+2)^2-4(2a+b+4)=0.
\]

So

\[
b=\frac{a^2-4a-12}{4}
=\frac{(a-2)^2}{4}-4.
\]

Hence \(a\) must be even. Let

\[
t=\frac{a-2}{2}.
\]

Then

\[
b=t^2-4.
\]

The bounds \(-20\le b\le20\) give

\[
|t|\le4.
\]

Thus \(t=-4,-3,\ldots,4\), giving \(9\) pairs \((a,b)\), except \(a=-6\) corresponds to the double root \(m=2\), which is not allowed.

So this case contributes

\[
8
\]

pairs \((a,b)\).

### Case 2: one root of \(q\) is \(2\), and the other is a distinct integer

Require

\[
q(2)=0.
\]

This gives

\[
4+2(a+2)+(2a+b+4)=0,
\]

hence

\[
b=-4a-12.
\]

The bound \(-20\le b\le20\) yields

\[
-8\le a\le2,
\]

giving \(11\) integer values of \(a\). Again exclude \(a=-6\), for which both roots are \(2\).

Thus this case contributes

\[
10
\]

pairs \((a,b)\).

Therefore the number of valid \((a,b)\) pairs is

\[
8+10=18.
\]

The coefficient \(c\) does not occur in \(p(m)-p(2)\), so it can be chosen arbitrarily in \(41\) ways.

Hence the total number of polynomials is

\[
18\cdot41=\boxed{738}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

When comparing polynomial values,

\[
p(m)=p(k),
\]

factor

\[
p(m)-p(k)
\]

by \(m-k\).

Here the cubic comparison reduces to a monic quadratic. Then use the key fact:

> A monic polynomial with integer coefficients that has one integer root has its remaining root integral as well (for a quadratic, by Vieta).

The phrase **“unique integer \(m\ne2\)”** forces a multiplicity/root-exclusion case split.

## Accuracy notes

- **Candidate 11:** \(c=0\) is rejected because \(x^3\) has only one distinct real root, not exactly two.
- **Candidate 12:** No division by \(x+y+z\) is needed; the derivation \(sq=3r\) remains valid even if one initially worries that \(s\) might be zero. Since \(r=xyz\ne0\), the resulting relation in fact forces \(s\ne0\).
- **Candidate 13:** The negative algebraic possibility \(a+b=-3\) is impossible because \(a,b>1\).
- **Candidate 14:** The exponent \(2023\) is essential because it is odd; the opposite reciprocal powers cancel.
- **Candidate 15:** Both mechanisms giving exactly one admissible integer \(m\ne2\) were counted, and the overlap case \(m=2\) double was explicitly excluded.
