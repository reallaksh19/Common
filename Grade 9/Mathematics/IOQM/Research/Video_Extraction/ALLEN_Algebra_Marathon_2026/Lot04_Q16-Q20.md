## Q16. Cubic system via the common product \(xyz\)

### Question

Let \((a,b,c)\) be a real solution of

\[
x^3-xyz=2,\qquad
y^3-xyz=6,\qquad
z^3-xyz=20.
\]

The greatest possible value of

\[
a^3+b^3+c^3
\]

can be written as \(\frac mn\), where \(m,n\) are relatively prime positive integers. Find \(m+n\).

**Answer:** \(\boxed{158}\)

**Original source identified:** 2010 AIME I, Problem 9.

### Short solution

Let

\[
p=xyz.
\]

Then

\[
x^3=p+2,\qquad y^3=p+6,\qquad z^3=p+20.
\]

Cubing \(xyz=p\),

\[
p^3=x^3y^3z^3=(p+2)(p+6)(p+20).
\]

Hence

\[
p^3=p^3+28p^2+172p+240,
\]

so

\[
28p^2+172p+240=0,
\]

or

\[
7p^2+43p+60=0.
\]

Thus

\[
p=-4\quad\text{or}\quad p=-\frac{15}{7}.
\]

Now

\[
x^3+y^3+z^3
=(p+2)+(p+6)+(p+20)
=3p+28.
\]

The two values are

\[
16
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{151}{7}.
\]

Therefore the greatest is

\[
\frac{151}{7},
\]

and

\[
m+n=151+7=\boxed{158}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

When several equations share the same product \(xyz\), set

\[
p=xyz.
\]

This can convert every cube into a linear expression in \(p\). Then use

\[
(xyz)^3=x^3y^3z^3
\]

to obtain a low-degree equation for \(p\), often with major cancellation.

## Q17. Extremal gap in an ordered zero-sum tuple

### Question

Let

\[
x_1\le x_2\le\cdots\le x_{100}
\]

be real numbers satisfying

\[
|x_1|+|x_2|+\cdots+|x_{100}|=1
\]

and

\[
x_1+x_2+\cdots+x_{100}=0.
\]

Among all such \(100\)-tuples, the greatest possible value of

\[
x_{76}-x_{16}
\]

is \(\frac mn\), where \(m,n\) are relatively prime positive integers. Find \(m+n\).

**Answer:** \(\boxed{841}\)

**Original source identified:** 2022 AIME II, Problem 6.

### Short solution

Because the total sum is \(0\) and the sum of absolute values is \(1\),

\[
\text{total positive mass}
=
\text{total negative absolute mass}
=
\frac12.
\]

Since

\[
x_{76}\le x_{77}\le\cdots\le x_{100},
\]

there are \(25\) terms at least \(x_{76}\). Therefore

\[
25x_{76}\le\frac12,
\]

so

\[
x_{76}\le\frac1{50}.
\]

Similarly, the first \(16\) terms are at most \(x_{16}\). If \(x_{16}<0\), their total negative absolute mass is at least \(16(-x_{16})\), so

\[
16(-x_{16})\le\frac12,
\]

hence

\[
x_{16}\ge-\frac1{32}.
\]

Therefore

\[
x_{76}-x_{16}
\le
\frac1{50}+\frac1{32}
=
\frac{41}{800}.
\]

Equality is achieved by taking

\[
x_1=\cdots=x_{16}=-\frac1{32},
\]

\[
x_{17}=\cdots=x_{75}=0,
\]

and

\[
x_{76}=\cdots=x_{100}=\frac1{50}.
\]

Thus

\[
m+n=41+800=\boxed{841}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

For ordered variables with

\[
\sum |x_i|=1,\qquad \sum x_i=0,
\]

the positive and negative masses are each exactly \(1/2\).

To bound an order statistic such as \(x_k\), count how many terms must be at least or at most that value. This is a very efficient extremal method.

## Q18. Recovering reciprocal sums from symmetric data

### Question

Suppose \(a,b,c\) are complex numbers satisfying

\[
a+b+c=6,
\]

\[
a^3+b^3+c^3=87,
\]

and

\[
(a+1)(b+1)(c+1)=33.
\]

If

\[
\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c=\frac mn
\]

for relatively prime positive integers \(m,n\), find \(m+n\).

**Answer:** \(\boxed{182}\)

### Short solution

Let

\[
s=a+b+c=6,\qquad
q=ab+bc+ca,\qquad
r=abc.
\]

Using

\[
a^3+b^3+c^3=s^3-3sq+3r,
\]

we get

\[
87=216-18q+3r,
\]

so

\[
r-6q=-43.
\]

Also,

\[
(a+1)(b+1)(c+1)
=r+q+s+1=33.
\]

Since \(s=6\),

\[
r+q=26.
\]

Solving

\[
r-6q=-43,\qquad r+q=26
\]

gives

\[
q=\frac{69}{7},
\qquad
r=\frac{113}{7}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c
=
\frac{ab+bc+ca}{abc}
=
\frac qr
=
\frac{69}{113}.
\]

Hence

\[
m+n=69+113=\boxed{182}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

For symmetric expressions in three variables, use

\[
s=a+b+c,\qquad
q=ab+bc+ca,\qquad
r=abc.
\]

Two especially useful identities are

\[
a^3+b^3+c^3=s^3-3sq+3r
\]

and

\[
\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c=\frac qr.
\]

Also expand shifted products such as

\[
(a+1)(b+1)(c+1)=r+q+s+1.
\]

## Q19. Squares of three arithmetic-progression terms

### Question

If the integer \(k\) is added to each of the numbers

\[
36,\quad300,\quad596,
\]

one obtains the squares of three consecutive terms of an arithmetic progression. Find \(k\).

**Answer:** \(\boxed{925}\)

**Original source identified:** 1989 AIME, Problem 7.

> **Terminology note:** The original AIME statement uses “arithmetic series,” but the intended mathematical object is an arithmetic progression/sequence.

### Short solution

Let the three consecutive AP terms be

\[
u-d,\qquad u,\qquad u+d.
\]

Then

\[
36+k=(u-d)^2,
\]

\[
300+k=u^2,
\]

\[
596+k=(u+d)^2.
\]

Subtract consecutive equations:

\[
264=u^2-(u-d)^2=2ud-d^2,
\]

\[
296=(u+d)^2-u^2=2ud+d^2.
\]

Subtract:

\[
32=2d^2,
\]

so

\[
d^2=16.
\]

Taking \(d=4\) without loss of generality, adding the two equations gives

\[
560=4ud,
\]

hence

\[
u=35.
\]

Therefore

\[
36+k=(35-4)^2=31^2=961,
\]

so

\[
k=961-36=\boxed{925}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

For three consecutive AP terms, use the symmetric parametrization

\[
u-d,\quad u,\quad u+d.
\]

After squaring, subtract adjacent equations. The asymmetric linear terms cancel when you subtract again, often revealing \(d^2\) immediately.

## Q20. Product of two arithmetic sequences

### Question

Find the eighth term of the sequence

\[
1440,\ 1716,\ 1848,\ldots
\]

whose terms are formed by multiplying corresponding terms of two arithmetic sequences.

**Answer:** \(\boxed{348}\)

**Original source identified:** 2003 AIME II, Problem 8.

### Short solution

The \(n\)-th term of an arithmetic sequence is linear in \(n\). Therefore the product of corresponding terms of two arithmetic sequences is quadratic in \(n\).

So the given sequence has constant second difference.

The first differences are

\[
1716-1440=276,
\]

\[
1848-1716=132.
\]

Thus the constant second difference is

\[
132-276=-144.
\]

Continue the first differences:

\[
276,\ 132,\ -12,\ -156,\ -300,\ -444,\ -588.
\]

Hence the terms continue as

\[
1440,\ 1716,\ 1848,\ 1836,\ 1680,\ 1380,\ 936,\ 348.
\]

Therefore the eighth term is

\[
\boxed{348}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

The product of two linear functions of \(n\) is quadratic in \(n\).

So whenever a sequence is defined as the termwise product of two arithmetic sequences, its **second finite difference is constant**. You usually do not need to reconstruct the two underlying APs.
