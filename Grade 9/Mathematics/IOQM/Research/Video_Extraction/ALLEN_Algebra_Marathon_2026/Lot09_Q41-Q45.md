## Q41. Cubics whose roots and coefficients are linked

### Question

How many cubics of the form

\[
x^3-ax^2+(a+d)x-(a+2d),
\]

where \(a,d\) are integers, have all roots nonnegative integers?

**Answer:** \(\boxed{5}\)

### Short solution

Let the roots be

\[
0\le r\le s\le t.
\]

By Vieta,

\[
r+s+t=a,
\]

\[
rs+rt+st=a+d,
\]

\[
rst=a+2d.
\]

Eliminate \(a,d\). Since

\[
d=(rs+rt+st)-(r+s+t),
\]

we obtain

\[
rst
=
2(rs+rt+st)-(r+s+t).
\tag{1}
\]

### Case \(r=0\)

Equation (1) gives

\[
0=2st-s-t.
\]

This yields only

\[
(r,s,t)=(0,0,0),(0,1,1).
\]

### Case \(r>0\)

Dividing (1) by \(rst\),

\[
1
=
2\left(\frac1r+\frac1s+\frac1t\right)
-
\left(\frac1{rs}+\frac1{rt}+\frac1{st}\right).
\]

Since the subtracted terms are positive,

\[
1<\frac6r,
\]

so

\[
r\le5.
\]

For \(r=1,2\), solving (1) for \(t\) gives no positive \(t\ge s\).

For \(r\ge3\),

\[
t=
\frac{2rs-r-s}{rs-2r-2s+1}.
\]

A short finite check subject to \(t\ge s\) gives:

- \(r=3\): \((s,t)=(6,27),(7,16)\);
- \(r=4\): \((s,t)=(4,24)\);
- \(r=5\): none.

Thus the five root multisets are

\[
(0,0,0),\quad
(0,1,1),\quad
(3,6,27),\quad
(3,7,16),\quad
(4,4,24).
\]

Each root multiset determines one monic cubic, so the answer is

\[
\boxed5.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

If polynomial coefficients are algebraically linked, write Vieta first and **eliminate the coefficients**. This turns the problem into a Diophantine equation in the roots, where ordering and reciprocal bounds can make the search finite.

## Q42. Quartic with exactly one distinct real root

### Question

Find all real numbers \(a\) such that

\[
x^4+ax^3+1
\]

has exactly one real root.

**Answer:**

\[
\boxed{a=\pm\frac{4}{\sqrt[4]{27}}}
\]

equivalently,

\[
\boxed{a=\pm\frac{4}{3^{3/4}}}.
\]

### Short solution

Let

\[
f(x)=x^4+ax^3+1.
\]

Since a real quartic with positive leading coefficient tends to \(+\infty\) at both ends, exactly one distinct real zero must occur when its global minimum just touches the \(x\)-axis.

Differentiate:

\[
f'(x)=x^2(4x+3a).
\]

The point \(x=0\) does not change the sign of \(f'\), and

\[
f(0)=1.
\]

Thus the actual minimum occurs at

\[
r=-\frac{3a}{4}.
\]

For exactly one real root, require

\[
f(r)=0.
\]

Using \(r=-3a/4\), note

\[
r+a=\frac a4.
\]

Hence

\[
f(r)=r^3(r+a)+1
=
\left(-\frac{3a}{4}\right)^3\frac a4+1
=
1-\frac{27a^4}{256}.
\]

Therefore

\[
a^4=\frac{256}{27},
\]

so

\[
a=\pm\frac{4}{\sqrt[4]{27}}.
\]

For these values the minimum is a double zero and all other points have positive value, so there is exactly one distinct real root.

### IOQM tip / trick

For an even-degree polynomial that tends to \(+\infty\) at both ends, “exactly one real root” usually means a tangent root at the global minimum:

\[
f(r)=0,\qquad f'(r)=0.
\]

Do not merely solve the discriminant condition without checking the global shape.

## Q43. Turn the given equation into the target expression

### Question

Let \(z\) be a complex number satisfying

\[
\frac{z-4}{z^2-5z+1}
+
\frac{2z-4}{2z^2-5z+1}
+
\frac{z-2}{z^2-3z+1}
=
\frac3z.
\]

Over all possible values of \(z\), find the sum of the possible values of

\[
\left|
\frac1{z^2-5z+1}
+
\frac1{2z^2-5z+1}
+
\frac1{z^2-3z+1}
\right|.
\]

**Answer:** \(\boxed{\frac{11}{6}}\)

**Original source identified:** 2022 CMIMC, problem proposed by Justin Hsieh.

> **Notation correction:** Absolute-value bars in the target are restored from the original source.

### Short solution

Let

\[
A=z^2-5z+1,\quad
B=2z^2-5z+1,\quad
C=z^2-3z+1.
\]

Multiply the given equation by \(z\):

\[
\frac{z(z-4)}A+\frac{z(2z-4)}B+\frac{z(z-2)}C=3.
\]

Subtract \(1+1+1\). Since

\[
z(z-4)-A=z-1,
\]

\[
z(2z-4)-B=z-1,
\]

and

\[
z(z-2)-C=z-1,
\]

we get

\[
(z-1)
\left(
\frac1A+\frac1B+\frac1C
\right)=0.
\]

Therefore either:

- \(z=1\), or
- the target inner sum is \(0\).

At \(z=1\),

\[
A=-3,\quad B=-2,\quad C=-1,
\]

so the absolute value is

\[
\left|-\frac13-\frac12-1\right|
=
\frac{11}{6}.
\]

All other admissible solutions contribute target value \(0\).

Hence the sum of all possible target values is

\[
0+\frac{11}{6}
=
\boxed{\frac{11}{6}}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

A contest equation is often engineered so that, after multiplying by a simple factor and subtracting constants, **each numerator becomes the same factor**. Here all three become \(z-1\), directly exposing the expression being asked for.

## Q44. Composition of monic quadratics

### Question

Let \(P(x),Q(x),R(x)\) be monic quadratic polynomials with only real roots and satisfying

\[
P(Q(x))=(x-1)(x-3)(x-5)(x-7),
\]

\[
Q(R(x))=(x-2)(x-4)(x-6)(x-8)
\]

for all real \(x\).

Find

\[
P(0)+Q(0)+R(0).
\]

**Answer:** \(\boxed{129}\)

**Original source identified:** 2021 CMIMC, Problem 6.

### Short solution

Write

\[
Q(x)=x^2+ux+v.
\]

Since \(P(Q(x))\) vanishes at \(1,3,5,7\), those four inputs must map under the quadratic \(Q\) to the two roots of \(P\).

For a quadratic,

\[
Q(r)=Q(s),\ r\ne s
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
r+s=-u.
\]

The only pairing of \(\{1,3,5,7\}\) into two pairs with the same sum is

\[
(1,7),\quad(3,5),
\]

both summing to \(8\). Thus

\[
u=-8
\]

and

\[
Q(x)=x^2-8x+v.
\]

Then

\[
Q(1)=Q(7)=v-7,\qquad
Q(3)=Q(5)=v-15,
\]

so

\[
P(y)=(y-v+7)(y-v+15).
\]

Now

\[
Q(R(x))
=
(R(x)-4)^2+v-16.
\]

Also

\[
(x-2)(x-4)(x-6)(x-8)
=
\bigl((x-5)^2-5\bigr)^2-16.
\]

Because \(R\) is monic quadratic, comparison forces

\[
R(x)-4=(x-5)^2-5
\]

and

\[
v=0.
\]

Therefore

\[
Q(0)=0,
\]

\[
P(0)=7\cdot15=105,
\]

and

\[
R(x)=x^2-10x+24,
\quad R(0)=24.
\]

Thus

\[
P(0)+Q(0)+R(0)
=
105+0+24
=
\boxed{129}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

For compositions \(P(Q(x))\), roots of the composition are **preimages under \(Q\)** of roots of \(P\).

For a quadratic \(Q\), equal output values occur at points symmetric about its axis, so paired inputs have a constant sum. This often determines the linear coefficient of \(Q\) immediately.

**Problem and answer:** Fully verified from the 2021 CMIMC source.

## Q45. Minimum sum of squares from complementary pair products

### Question

Let \(a,b,c,d\) be positive real numbers satisfying

\[
(a+b)(c+d)=143,
\]

\[
(a+c)(b+d)=150,
\]

\[
(a+d)(b+c)=169.
\]

Find the smallest possible value of

\[
a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2.
\]

**Answer:** \(\boxed{214}\)

### Short solution

Let

\[
S=a+b+c+d.
\]

Add the three given equations. Every pairwise product appears exactly twice:

\[
2(ab+ac+ad+bc+bd+cd)
=
143+150+169
=
462.
\]

Hence

\[
a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2
=
S^2-462.
\]

Now

\[
(a+d)+(b+c)=S
\]

and

\[
(a+d)(b+c)=169.
\]

By AM-GM,

\[
S^2\ge4\cdot169=676.
\]

Therefore

\[
a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2
\ge
676-462
=
214.
\]

It remains to verify attainability. Take

\[
a=\frac{13+\sqrt{26}+\sqrt{19}}2,
\]

\[
b=\frac{13+\sqrt{26}-\sqrt{19}}2,
\]

\[
c=\frac{13-\sqrt{26}+\sqrt{19}}2,
\]

\[
d=\frac{13-\sqrt{26}-\sqrt{19}}2.
\]

All four are positive, and

\[
(a+d)(b+c)=13^2=169,
\]

\[
(a+b)(c+d)
=
(13+\sqrt{26})(13-\sqrt{26})
=143,
\]

\[
(a+c)(b+d)
=
(13+\sqrt{19})(13-\sqrt{19})
=150.
\]

Thus equality is attainable and the minimum is

\[
\boxed{214}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

If complementary pair sums such as

\[
a+b,\quad c+d
\]

appear in products, introduce the total

\[
S=a+b+c+d.
\]

Then each pair of complementary sums has total \(S\), so AM-GM gives a direct lower bound on \(S\). Also use

\[
\sum a^2=S^2-2\sum_{i<j}a_ia_j.
\]

Always provide an equality construction for an extremum.
