## Q21. Comparing a geometric series with its squared series

### Question

An infinite geometric series has sum \(2005\). A new series, obtained by squaring each term of the original series, has sum \(10\) times the sum of the original series.

The common ratio of the original series is \(\frac mn\), where \(m,n\) are relatively prime positive integers. Find \(m+n\).

**Answer:** \(\boxed{802}\)

**Original source identified:** 2005 AIME II, Problem 3.

### Short solution

Let the original series have first term \(a\) and common ratio \(r\). Then

\[
\frac{a}{1-r}=2005.
\]

The squared series has first term \(a^2\), ratio \(r^2\), and sum

\[
\frac{a^2}{1-r^2}=20050.
\]

But

\[
\frac{a^2}{1-r^2}
=
\frac{a}{1-r}\cdot\frac{a}{1+r}.
\]

Therefore

\[
2005\cdot\frac{a}{1+r}
=
10\cdot2005,
\]

so

\[
\frac{a}{1+r}=10.
\]

Hence

\[
a=2005(1-r)=10(1+r).
\]

Thus

\[
2005-2005r=10+10r,
\]

so

\[
1995=2015r
\]

and

\[
r=\frac{399}{403}.
\]

Therefore

\[
m+n=399+403=\boxed{802}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

When a geometric series and the series of its squared terms are both given, exploit

\[
1-r^2=(1-r)(1+r).
\]

This lets you divide the two sum formulas cleanly instead of solving for the first term directly.

## Q22. Odd-number increments in an arithmetic sequence

### Question

The terms of an arithmetic sequence add to \(715\). The first term is increased by \(1\), the second by \(3\), the third by \(5\), and in general the \(k\)-th term is increased by the \(k\)-th odd positive integer.

The terms of the new sequence add to \(836\).

Find the sum of the first, last, and middle terms of the original sequence.

**Answer:** \(\boxed{195}\)

**Original source identified:** 2012 AIME I, Problem 2.

### Short solution

Suppose the original sequence has \(n\) terms.

The total increase is

\[
836-715=121.
\]

But the sum of the first \(n\) odd positive integers is

\[
1+3+\cdots+(2n-1)=n^2.
\]

Hence

\[
n^2=121,
\]

so

\[
n=11.
\]

For an arithmetic sequence with \(11\) terms, the middle term equals the average:

\[
\frac{715}{11}=65.
\]

Also the first and last terms have average \(65\), so

\[
a_1+a_{11}=130.
\]

Therefore

\[
a_1+a_6+a_{11}
=
130+65
=
\boxed{195}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

Always recognize

\[
1+3+5+\cdots+(2n-1)=n^2.
\]

For an odd-length arithmetic progression, the middle term is simultaneously:

- the average of all terms;
- the average of the first and last terms.

That can eliminate the need to determine the common difference.

## Q23. Two geometric series with the same second term

### Question

Two distinct real infinite geometric series each have sum \(1\) and have the same second term. The third term of one of the series is

\[
\frac18.
\]

The common second term can be written as

\[
\frac{\sqrt m-n}{p},
\]

where \(m,n,p\) are positive integers and \(m\) is not divisible by the square of any prime.

Find

\[
100m+10n+p.
\]

**Answer:** \(\boxed{518}\)

**Original source identified:** 2002 AIME II, Problem 11.

> **Notation restoration:** The worksheet text extraction flattens the radical. The published problem confirms the form \(\frac{\sqrt m-n}{p}\).

### Short solution

Let one series have ratio \(r\). Since its sum is \(1\), its first term is

\[
1-r.
\]

Hence its second term is

\[
r(1-r).
\]

If the other series has ratio \(s\), equality of second terms gives

\[
r(1-r)=s(1-s).
\]

Therefore

\[
(r-s)(1-r-s)=0.
\]

The series are distinct, so \(r\ne s\), hence

\[
r+s=1.
\]

The third term of one series is

\[
(1-r)r^2=\frac18.
\]

Thus

\[
8r^2(1-r)=1,
\]

or

\[
8r^3-8r^2+1=0.
\]

Factor:

\[
(2r-1)(4r^2-2r-1)=0.
\]

The root \(r=\frac12\) would make the two series identical, so discard it.

The admissible convergent choice is

\[
r=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{4}.
\]

Therefore the common second term is

\[
r(1-r)
=
\frac{\sqrt5-1}{8}.
\]

Thus

\[
m=5,\qquad n=1,\qquad p=8,
\]

and

\[
100m+10n+p
=
500+10+8
=
\boxed{518}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

If an infinite geometric series has sum \(1\) and ratio \(r\), its first few terms are

\[
1-r,\quad r(1-r),\quad r^2(1-r),\ldots
\]

Two ratios with the same second term satisfy

\[
r(1-r)=s(1-s),
\]

which factors immediately into

\[
(r-s)(1-r-s)=0.
\]

For distinct series, the ratios must add to \(1\).

## Q24. A five-step sign reversal in a recurrence

### Question

Given

\[
x_1=211,\qquad
x_2=375,\qquad
x_3=420,\qquad
x_4=523,
\]

and

\[
x_n=x_{n-1}-x_{n-2}+x_{n-3}-x_{n-4}
\qquad(n\ge5),
\]

find

\[
x_{531}+x_{753}+x_{975}.
\]

**Answer:** \(\boxed{898}\)

**Original source identified:** 2001 AIME II, Problem 3.

### Short solution

For \(n\ge6\),

\[
x_{n-1}
=
x_{n-2}-x_{n-3}+x_{n-4}-x_{n-5}.
\]

Substitute this into the recurrence for \(x_n\):

\[
x_n
=
-x_{n-5}.
\]

Hence

\[
x_{n+10}=x_n,
\]

so the sequence has period \(10\).

Compute

\[
x_5
=
523-420+375-211
=
267.
\]

Now

\[
531\equiv1\pmod{10},
\]

\[
753\equiv3\pmod{10},
\]

\[
975\equiv5\pmod{10}.
\]

Therefore

\[
x_{531}=x_1=211,
\]

\[
x_{753}=x_3=420,
\]

\[
x_{975}=x_5=267.
\]

Thus

\[
211+420+267=\boxed{898}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

For a linear recurrence, do not assume a period from a few numerical terms.

Instead, combine shifted copies of the recurrence algebraically. Here substitution gives the exact relation

\[
x_n=-x_{n-5},
\]

and therefore rigorously

\[
x_{n+10}=x_n.
\]

This is much safer than pattern-spotting alone.

## Q25. Cyclic rational expressions

### Question

Suppose \(a,b,c\) are real numbers for which the displayed fractions are defined and

\[
\frac{ac}{a+b}
+
\frac{ab}{b+c}
+
\frac{bc}{c+a}
=
-9
\]

and

\[
\frac{bc}{a+b}
+
\frac{ca}{b+c}
+
\frac{ab}{c+a}
=
10.
\]

Compute

\[
\frac{b}{a+b}
+
\frac{c}{b+c}
+
\frac{a}{c+a}.
\]

**Answer:** \(\boxed{11}\)

> **Reconstruction note:** The worksheet extraction renders the cyclic numerators in a compressed layout. The form above is the mathematically consistent restoration and is independently verified against the worksheet answer \(11\).

### Short solution

Let

\[
s=a+b+c.
\]

Define

\[
T=
\frac{b}{a+b}
+
\frac{c}{b+c}
+
\frac{a}{c+a},
\]

and

\[
U=
\frac{a}{a+b}
+
\frac{b}{b+c}
+
\frac{c}{c+a}.
\]

Termwise,

\[
T+U=3.
\]

Now

\[
s\frac{b}{a+b}
=
b+\frac{bc}{a+b},
\]

and similarly for the other cyclic terms. Hence

\[
sT
=
s+
\left(
\frac{bc}{a+b}
+
\frac{ca}{b+c}
+
\frac{ab}{c+a}
\right)
=
s+10.
\]

Likewise,

\[
sU
=
s+
\left(
\frac{ac}{a+b}
+
\frac{ab}{b+c}
+
\frac{bc}{c+a}
\right)
=
s-9.
\]

Adding,

\[
s(T+U)=2s+1.
\]

Since \(T+U=3\),

\[
3s=2s+1,
\]

so

\[
s=1.
\]

Then

\[
T=\frac{s+10}{s}=11.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{11}.
\]

### IOQM tip / trick

When denominators are pairwise sums such as

\[
a+b,\quad b+c,\quad c+a,
\]

multiply the target by

\[
s=a+b+c.
\]

For example,

\[
s\frac{b}{a+b}
=
b+\frac{bc}{a+b}.
\]

This transforms a degree-\(0\) rational target into the degree-\(1\) cyclic expressions already supplied in the hypotheses.

Also look for complementary fractions:

\[
\frac{a}{a+b}+\frac{b}{a+b}=1.
\]
