# Appendix A — Attached Q1–Q56 practice set

**Instructions:** The questions below are reproduced from the supplied study compilation as a clean practice set. Source/module notes and worked solutions have been removed. Do not check the answer key until you have made a complete attempt.

## Q1. Pairing the integers 1 through 16

How many ways are there to split the integers \(1\) through \(16\) into \(8\) pairs such that, in each pair, the greater number is at least twice the lesser number?

## Q2. Hugs and handshakes

At a gathering of \(30\) people, there are \(20\) people who all know each other and \(10\) people who know no one. People who know each other hug, and people who do not know each other shake hands. How many handshakes occur within the group?

## Q3. Subsets containing a prime

How many subsets of

\[
\{2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9\}
\]

contain at least one prime number?

## Q4. Four-digit integers containing 2 or 3

How many four-digit positive integers have at least one digit that is a \(2\) or a \(3\)?

## Q5. Integer angles in arithmetic progression

How many non-similar triangles have angles whose degree measures are distinct positive integers in arithmetic progression?

## Q6. Restricted arrangements of five A's, B's, and C's

How many 15-letter arrangements of 5 A's, 5 B's, and 5 C's have no A's in the first 5 letters, no B's in the next 5 letters, and no C's in the last 5 letters?

## Q7. When do two parabolas intersect?

Values for \(A,B,C,D\) are selected from

\[
\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}
\]

without replacement. How many choices make the curves

\[
y=Ax^2+B
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
y=Cx^2+D
\]

intersect?

The order of the two curves does not matter.

## Q8. Four crops in a \(2\times2\) field

A rectangular field is partitioned into a \(2\times2\) grid. Each of its four sections is planted with one of four crops:

- corn,
- wheat,
- soybeans,
- potatoes.

Corn and wheat may not be planted in two sections sharing a border. Soybeans and potatoes may likewise not be planted in two sections sharing a border.

How many valid crop assignments are there?

## Q9. Three brother-sister pairs in two rows

Three young brother-sister pairs from different families take a trip in a van. The six children occupy two rows of three seats each.

To avoid disruptions:

1. siblings may not sit next to each other in the same row; and
2. no child may sit directly in front of his or her sibling.

How many seating arrangements are possible?

## Q10. Positive-power terms in a multinomial expansion

For some particular value of \(N\), when

\[
(a+b+c+d+1)^N
\]

is expanded and like terms are combined, the resulting expression contains exactly \(1001\) terms that include all four variables \(a,b,c,d\), each raised to a positive power. What is \(N\)?

## Q11. Sharing 24 apples with lower bounds

Alice has \(24\) apples. In how many ways can she share them with Becky and Chris so that each of the three people has at least \(2\) apples?

## Q12. Binary strings avoiding \(101\) and \(010\)

How many binary strings of length \(10\) are there that contain neither \(101\) nor \(010\) as a substring?

## Q13. Four-digit snakelike integers

An integer is called **snakelike** if its decimal representation

\[
a_1a_2\cdots a_k
\]

satisfies

\[
a_i<a_{i+1}\quad\text{when }i\text{ is odd},
\]

and

\[
a_i>a_{i+1}\quad\text{when }i\text{ is even}.
\]

How many snakelike integers between \(1000\) and \(9999\) have four distinct digits?

## Q14. Permutations with no stable proper prefix

Find the number of permutations of

\[
1,2,3,4,5,6
\]

such that for every integer \(k\) with

\[
1\le k\le5,
\]

at least one of the first \(k\) terms of the permutation is greater than \(k\).

## Q15. Permutations with no monotone run of length three

In how many ways can the sequence

\[
1,2,3,4,5
\]

be rearranged so that no three consecutive terms are increasing and no three consecutive terms are decreasing?

## Q16. Ratio of two nonlinear recurrences

The sequence \(\{a_n\}\) is defined by

\[
a_0=1,\qquad a_1=1,
\]

and, for \(n\ge2\),

\[
a_n=a_{n-1}+\frac{a_{n-1}^2}{a_{n-2}}.
\]

The sequence \(\{b_n\}\) is defined by

\[
b_0=1,\qquad b_1=3,
\]

and, for \(n\ge2\),

\[
b_n=b_{n-1}+\frac{b_{n-1}^2}{b_{n-2}}.
\]

Find

\[
\frac{b_{32}}{a_{32}}.
\]

## Q17. Choosing six horses with three paired restrictions

A stud contains \(16\) horses. Among them are six special horses
\[
A,B,C,A',B',C',
\]
forming the three pairs \((A,A'),(B,B'),(C,C')\).

A team of \(6\) horses must contain exactly three of these six special horses, but it may not contain both members of any special pair. If the number of possible teams is \(k\), find
\[
\left\lfloor \frac{k}{12}\right\rfloor.
\]

## Q18. Committee with conditional membership

A committee of \(2\) women and \(3\) men is to be chosen from \(5\) women and \(6\) men.

Two of the men are \(A\) and \(B\), and one of the women is \(C\). The restrictions are:

- \(A\) refuses to serve if \(B\) serves;
- \(B\) can serve only if \(C\) serves.

If the number of committees is \(4m\), find \(m\).

## Q19. Right-neighbour restrictions at a circular table

Six people \(A,B,C,D,E,F\) are seated around a circular table. In how many arrangements can this be done if:

- the person immediately to the right of \(A\) is either \(B\) or \(C\);
- the person immediately to the right of \(B\) is either \(C\) or \(D\)?

## Q20. Three girls and five boys around a circle

In how many ways can \(3\) girls and \(5\) boys be seated around a circular table so that:

1. no two girls sit together; and
2. between any two consecutive girls around the circle there are at most \(2\) boys?

If the number is \(N\), find
\[
\frac N{10}.
\]

## Q21. At least two of three friends adjacent on a circle

Seven people are seated around a circular table. Three of them are friends \(F_1,F_2,F_3\). The number of seatings in which at least two of \(F_1,F_2,F_3\) sit next to each other is \(K^2\). Find \(K\).

## Q22. Selecting six letters from FLABELLIFORM

In how many ways is it possible to select \(6\) letters, including at least one vowel, from the letters of
\[
\text{FLABELLIFORM}?
\]
Selections distinguish letter multiplicities but not positions of identical letters. If the number of selections is \(N\), find the sum of the digits of \(N\).

## Q23. Committee with a together-or-neither pair

A committee of \(5\) is chosen from \(9\) people. Two particular people \(A,B\) must either both serve or both stay out. Two other particular people \(C,D\) refuse to serve together. How many committees are possible?

## Q24. Selecting one or two books from each subject

There are \(5\) different Physics books, \(3\) different Chemistry books, and \(4\) different Mathematics books. In how many ways can books be selected if at least one and at most two books must be selected from each subject?

## Q25. Interviewing three mothers and their children

Three mothers each bring one child for a school-admission interview. The six people are interviewed one by one, with the condition that no child is interviewed before his or her own mother. How many interview orders are possible?

## Q26. Factor pairs of 7056

In how many ways can
\[
7056
\]
be written as a product of two positive integer factors, where the order of the two factors is ignored?

## Q27. Largest power of 24 dividing the product of all divisors

Let \(P\) be the product of all positive divisors of \(1440\). If \(24^x\mid P\), find the greatest possible value of \(x\).

## Q28. Relay order with forbidden first and last runners

Five students \(A,B,C,D,E\) run a five-leg relay, one student per leg. \(A\) cannot run the first leg and \(D\) cannot run the last leg. How many running orders are possible?

## Q29. Divisors lost when lowering an exponent

Let \(N\) be the number of positive divisors of
\[
998^{49999}
\]
that are not divisors of
\[
998^{49998}.
\]
Find the sum of the digits of \(N\).

## Q30. Arrangements of HONOLULU with no equal adjacent letters

Let \(X\) be the number of distinct arrangements of the letters of
\[
\text{HONOLULU}
\]
in which no two identical letters are adjacent. Find the sum of the digits of \(X\).

## Q31. Avoiding three forbidden consecutive blocks

Let \(N\) be the number of permutations of \(A,B,C,D,E,F\) such that:

- \(D\) does not immediately follow \(C\);
- \(E\) does not immediately follow \(D\);
- \(F\) does not immediately follow \(E\).

Find
\[
\frac N6.
\]

## Q32. Nationalities separated in a row

Ten distinct people consist of \(2\) Americans, \(2\) British, \(2\) Chinese, and one person each from four other nationalities. They sit in a row of \(10\) chairs. No two people of the same nationality may sit together.

If the number of arrangements is
\[
k\cdot 8!,
\]
find \(k\).

## Q33. Pairing people who know neighbours and opposites

Ten people stand equally spaced around a circle. Each person knows exactly the two adjacent people and the person directly opposite.

In how many ways can the \(10\) people be split into \(5\) unordered pairs so that the two people in every pair know each other?

## Q34. 2-regular handshake graphs on nine people

In a group of \(9\) people, each person shakes hands with exactly \(2\) other people. Let \(N\) be the number of possible handshake graphs on these \(9\) labeled people.

Find the remainder when \(N\) is divided by \(1000\).

## Q35. MAXIMUM with no adjacent consonants

How many distinct words can be formed from all the letters of
\[
\text{MAXIMUM}
\]
if no two consonants are adjacent?

## Q36. ARRANGE with neither A's nor R's adjacent

The letters of
\[
\text{ARRANGE}
\]
are arranged in all distinct ways. Neither the two \(A\)'s nor the two \(R\)'s may be adjacent.

If the number of valid arrangements is \(10K\), find \(K\).

## Q37. Colored balls with at least one broken color-block

There are \(2\) identical white balls, \(3\) identical red balls, and \(4\) green balls of different shades, so the four green balls are distinguishable.

They are arranged in a row. Let \(m\) be the number of arrangements in which **at least one ball is separated from the other balls of its color**, i.e. it is not the case that all white balls form one block, all red balls form one block, and all green balls form one block.

Find the sum of the digits of \(m\).

## Q38. Three-digit multiples of 3 from four allowed digits

How many three-digit numbers divisible by \(3\) can be formed using only the digits
\[
1,3,5,8,
\]
if repetition is allowed?

## Q39. Four-letter arrangements from EXAMINATION

Let \(N\) be the number of distinct four-letter arrangements that can be formed from the letters of
\[
\text{EXAMINATION},
\]
without using any letter more often than it occurs in the word.

Find the sum of the digits of \(N\).

## Q40. Three-letter words from PROPOSAL with a vowel in the middle

How many distinct three-letter words can be formed from the letters of
\[
\text{PROPOSAL}
\]
if the middle letter must be a vowel?

## Q41. Eight-digit multiple of 5 with exactly two adjacent equal pairs

Let \(n\) be the number of eight-digit numbers divisible by \(5\) that can be made using exactly the multiset
\[
1,1,2,2,3,3,4,5
\]
such that **exactly two** of the three equal pairs \(11,22,33\) occur adjacently.

Find
\[
n-48.
\]

## Q42. Three teachers separated by exactly two students

A class has three distinct teachers and six distinct students. They are seated in a row of \(9\) chairs so that there are exactly two students between each pair of **successive teachers in the row**.

If the number of arrangements is
\[
k(6!),
\]
find \(k\).

## Q43. Lexicographic rank of ZENITH

All permutations of the letters of
\[
\text{ZENITH}
\]
are listed in dictionary order. If the rank of ZENITH is \(k\), find
\[
k-600.
\]

## Q44. Four-digit numbers beginning with 1 and having exactly one repeated pair

Let \(N\) be the number of four-digit numbers beginning with \(1\) whose four digits contain exactly one repeated pair; equivalently, the multiplicity pattern is
\[
2,1,1.
\]

Find the arithmetic mean of the digits of \(N\).

## Q45. Lexicographic rank of PROPER

All distinct permutations of the letters of
\[
\text{PROPER}
\]
are listed in dictionary order. If the rank of PROPER is \(m\), find
\[
\frac m5.
\]

## Q46. Five numbered people around five distinct circular seats

Five persons wearing badges numbered
\[
1,2,3,4,5
\]
sit on five chairs around a circular table. No two persons whose badge numbers are consecutive may sit next to each other.

Two arrangements obtained by rotation are considered different.

How many arrangements are possible?

## Q47. Five at a round table and five at a rectangular table

Out of \(10\) distinct people, \(5\) are seated around a round table and the other \(5\) are seated at distinct positions at a rectangular table.

If the total number of arrangements is
\[
2(n!),
\]
find
\[
2n.
\]

## Q48. Three boys and three girls with same-sex neighbours for two specified people

Three boys and three girls sit around a circular table. A specified boy \(X\) does not want either neighbour to be a girl, and a specified girl \(Y\) does not want either neighbour to be a boy.

How many arrangements are possible?

## Q49. Three-color hats around a 5-cycle

Five fixed people \(A,B,C,D,E\) sit in a circle. Each receives a hat colored red, blue, or green.

How many hat assignments are possible if adjacent people must receive different colors?

## Q50. Circular arrangements of ALASKA

In how many distinct circular arrangements can all the letters of
\[
\text{ALASKA}
\]
be placed if clockwise and anticlockwise arrangements are considered different?

## Q51. Garlands from three flowers of each of two kinds

How many different garlands can be formed using \(3\) identical flowers of one kind and \(3\) identical flowers of another kind?

For a garland, arrangements equivalent by rotation or reflection are considered the same.

## Q52. Ten students and a teacher around thirteen chairs

Ten distinct students and their teacher Mr. \(X\) sit around a circular table surrounded by \(13\) chairs, leaving two chairs empty.

Neither chair adjacent to Mr. \(X\) may be empty.

If the number of seatings is
\[
K(10!),
\]
find \(K\).

## Q53. Painting a cube with six different face colors

Six different colors are available, and every face of a cube is painted a different color.

Two colorings are considered the same if one can be rotated to coincide with the other.

How many different colorings are possible?

## Q54. Subsets whose size is not one of their elements

Let \(N\) be the number of nonempty subsets \(A\) of
\[
\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8\}
\]
such that, if \(|A|=k\), then
\[
k\notin A.
\]

Find the sum of the squares of the digits of \(N\).

## Q55. Six-letter words from HUDHUDBIRD using at most three distinct letters

Let \(N\) be the number of distinct six-letter words that can be formed from the letters of
\[
\text{HUDHUDBIRD}
\]
without exceeding the available multiplicities, if each word uses at most three distinct letters.

Find the sum of the digits of \(N\).

## Q56. Selecting colored balls with at least one green and one blue

There are \(5\) distinct green balls, \(4\) distinct blue balls, and \(3\) distinct red balls.

A group is formed containing at least one green ball and at least one blue ball; red balls are optional.

If the number of groups is
\[
10m,
\]
find the sum of the digits of \(m\).
