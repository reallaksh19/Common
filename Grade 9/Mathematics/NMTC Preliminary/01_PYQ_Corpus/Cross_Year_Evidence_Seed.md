## Mechanism recurrence already visible

The following are strong **candidate recurring families** because related mechanisms are visible across multiple ingested years. Exact counts must wait for solution-checked schema normalization.

### 1. Transform before calculate — very strong

Observed repeatedly through:

- reduction of high powers using a low-degree relation;
- radical/common-basis simplification;
- reciprocal linearization;
- logarithmic-variable substitution;
- exponential normalization;
- algebraic identity collapse;
- function shift/composition simplification.

Curriculum consequence: every algebra chapter needs explicit `TRANSFORM` and `FIRST MOVE` training, not formula recall alone.

### 2. Polynomial/root structure — very strong

Observed families include:

- factor/remainder reduction;
- divisibility by a polynomial;
- common roots;
- Vieta without explicit root solving;
- integer-root restrictions;
- repeated-root/discriminant conditions;
- perfect-power coefficient matching.

Curriculum consequence: Quadratics, higher-degree equations, factor/remainder theorem and Vieta should be connected as one structural network rather than isolated lessons.

### 3. Radical / exponent / logarithm compression — strong

Radical identities and exponential transformations occur across the corpus; explicit logarithm questions are especially visible in recent 2024–2025 reproductions.

Curriculum consequence: teach representation switching and substitution triggers, with a separate recognition drill for `same base`, `conjugate`, `t+1/t`, `log variable`, and `nested radical` forms.

### 4. Remainder / divisibility / modular thinking — strong

Observed through:

- common remainder -> LCM;
- common remainder -> GCD of differences;
- direct modular powers/squares;
- last-digit cycles;
- polynomial remainders;
- digit divisibility;
- integer-valued rational expressions.

Curriculum consequence: modular arithmetic must connect to digit problems, cycles, divisibility and polynomial-remainder reasoning instead of being taught as notation only.

### 5. Geometry recognition chains — strong

Every ingested year contains short objective geometry requiring rapid recognition of configurations such as:

- tangent/radius/diameter relations;
- cyclic or circle-angle chains;
- square/rhombus/parallel structures;
- equal-distance/equal-length geometry;
- median/altitude metric relations;
- inscribed/tangent circle geometry.

Many such questions are image-dependent. Their final archetypes remain blocked until figures are recovered and checked.

Curriculum consequence: geometry requires diagram-recognition cards and first-construction/first-mark drills, not theorem lists.

### 6. Sequence/accumulation structures — recurring

Observed through:

- recurrence transformation;
- consecutive block sums via prefix residues;
- weighted sums;
- triangular-number bounds;
- GP parameter recovery;
- infinite GP constraints.

Curriculum consequence: the existing Sequence & Series concept architecture should gain a Preliminary layer driven by these PYQ mechanisms.

### 7. Counting appears in disguised forms

Observed through:

- digit counting under divisibility/parity restrictions;
- connected-shape counting;
- grid/path counting;
- subset-product expansion;
- geometric-configuration counting.

Curriculum consequence: Fundamental Counting Principle must include modeling and case construction, not only permutation/combination formulas.
