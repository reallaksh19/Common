# Circle & Tangent Recognition — Concept Book Specification

## Purpose

Teach the learner to convert a diagram into a short chain of forced facts before performing calculations.

## Cognitive spine

`SEE -> MARK -> RELATE -> CHOOSE -> CHAIN -> CHECK -> ADOPT`

### SEE

Identify:

- centre(s);
- radius lines;
- tangent points;
- chord/arc endpoints;
- diameter;
- cyclic four-point sets;
- parallel lines;
- equal-length marks;
- external secants/tangents.

### MARK

Write only facts forced by the diagram/givens:

- radius ⟂ tangent;
- radii of same circle equal;
- tangents from same external point equal;
- diameter subtends 90°;
- equal chords ↔ equal central angles/arcs when applicable;
- cyclic opposite angles supplementary;
- same-chord/same-segment angles equal.

### RELATE

Ask which relation turns the target into one unknown:

- centre angle = twice inscribed angle on same arc;
- alternate segment theorem;
- intersecting chord product;
- secant/tangent power;
- similarity/Pythagoras after right-angle marking;
- equal tangent lengths;
- parallel-angle transfer.

### CHOOSE

Do not use every theorem. Choose the shortest relation that touches the target.

### CHAIN

Prefer 2–5 justified steps over broad angle chasing.

### CHECK

- Did you assume the drawing is to scale?
- Did you use the same arc/chord when equating angles?
- Did you confuse tangent-chord angle with central angle?
- Did you multiply the correct internal/external chord or secant segments?
- Is the target angle plausible (<180° where required)?
- Does the metric result have the correct unit/dimension?

## Core concept sequence

### C0 — circle vocabulary and visual grammar

Centre, radius, diameter, chord, arc, secant, tangent, point of contact.

Mastery condition: learner can name objects from a diagram without theorem prompts.

### C1 — radius and tangent

Invariant:

`radius to point of contact ⟂ tangent`.

Derive/justify geometrically as the shortest-distance condition from centre to the tangent line.

Contrast:

A radius to an arbitrary point is not perpendicular to an arbitrary secant.

### C2 — equal tangents from an external point

If tangents from P touch at A and B, then `PA=PB`.

Derive via right triangles `OPA`, `OPB` with common hypotenuse `OP` and equal radii.

Transfer to:

- perimeter relations;
- isosceles angle consequences;
- tangent-length algebra.

### C3 — centre and circumference angles

For same arc AB:

`∠AOB = 2∠ACB`.

Teach the arc/chord custody explicitly: the two angles must stand on the same endpoints/arc.

### C4 — same segment

Angles subtended by the same chord at points on the same segment are equal.

Contrast with points on opposite segments, where supplementary structure appears.

### C5 — diameter / semicircle

Angle in a semicircle is 90°.

Teach as direct consequence of C3 for a 180° central angle.

### C6 — cyclic quadrilateral

Opposite angles sum to 180°.

Recognition triggers:

- four points explicitly on a circle;
- opposite angles supplementary can prove cyclicity;
- equal subtended angles can prove cyclicity.

### C7 — tangent-chord / alternate segment theorem

Angle between tangent and chord equals the angle in the alternate segment subtended by that chord.

Mandatory correspondence discipline:

- identify tangent point;
- identify the chord;
- name the remote angle using the same chord endpoints.

### C8 — intersecting chords

If chords AB and CD intersect at X inside the circle:

`XA·XB = XC·XD`.

Teach through similarity, not as a naked formula.

### C9 — secant/tangent power

For external P:

- two secants: `(external)(whole) = (external)(whole)`;
- tangent + secant: `PT² = PA·PB` where A is near and B far along the secant.

Contrast internal chord product vs external secant product.

### C10 — tangent + parallel transfer

When a parallel is present, transfer the tangent/chord angle first; then use the circle theorem. This family is especially important for Preliminary recognition speed.

### C11 — metric circle geometry

After forced right angles are marked, use:

- Pythagoras;
- similarity;
- tangent equality;
- power of a point;
- centre distances for tangent circles.

### C12 — tangent-circle scale / homothety bridge

For circles tangent to both sides of a fixed angle, centres lie on the angle bisector and radii scale with distance from the vertex. Treat as a bridge family; do not force homothety language before the geometry is understood.

### C13 — source integrity

A geometry item is not canonically reproducible from an answer key alone.

Students should learn that:

- a missing diagram is missing mathematical data;
- a mislabeled target cannot be silently corrected;
- a non-scale drawing must not be measured;
- a source conflict is an evidence problem, not an invitation to guess.

## Mandatory contrast pairs

1. tangent vs secant;
2. central angle vs inscribed angle;
3. same segment vs cyclic supplementary;
4. internal intersecting chords vs external secants;
5. tangent-chord theorem vs radius-tangent 90°;
6. exact geometric fact vs visual appearance.

## First-move mastery

Before calculation, student must be able to state one of:

- `MARK RADIUS ⟂ TANGENT`
- `MARK EQUAL TANGENTS`
- `SAME ARC: CENTRE=2×CIRCUMFERENCE`
- `SAME CHORD: EQUAL ANGLES`
- `DIAMETER -> 90°`
- `CYCLIC -> SUPPLEMENTARY`
- `TANGENT-CHORD -> ALTERNATE SEGMENT`
- `INTERSECTING CHORD PRODUCT`
- `POWER OF POINT`
- `TRANSFER PARALLEL ANGLE FIRST`
- `RIGHT TRIANGLE / SIMILARITY AFTER MARKING`
- `FIGURE CUSTODY BLOCK`

## Publication gates for this concept book

- no naked theorem list without derivation/visual reason;
- every theorem gets a wrong-trigger contrast;
- all author-created problems use unambiguous textual geometry;
- historical figure-gated items remain references, not reconstructed drawings;
- recognition and first-line drills exist;
- non-identical transfer exists;
- mixed test hides theorem labels.
