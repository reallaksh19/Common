# Issue #49 — Wave 1D Interface: Weighted Sums

`STREAM: W1-D`

`STATUS: PASS_INTERNAL`

## 1. CONCEPT_SCOPE

Owns weighted polynomial sums, explicit kth-term construction, sum linearity, nested multiplicity counting, standard power-sum reduction, and finite weighted-geometric shift/subtract patterns.

## 2. PREREQUISITES

- sigma meaning as repeated addition;
- polynomial expansion;
- standard `sum k` and `sum k^2` tools;
- finite GP shift/subtract idea;
- index bounds.

## 3. LIKELY_HALF_KNOWLEDGE

Learner sees an indexed list and tries to classify it as AP/GP, or uses standard sums without first constructing the actual kth term. Nested sums are often expanded by brute force rather than counted structurally.

## 4. RECOGNITION_CUES

- kth term contains `k`, `k^2`, or another polynomial weight;
- a sum contains another sum inside it;
- each underlying term appears multiple times with a predictable multiplicity;
- coefficients `1,2,3,...` multiply geometric powers.

## 5. FIRST_MOVES

1. Write the general summand `T_k` explicitly.
2. Expand only enough to expose standard pieces.
3. Use summation linearity.
4. For nested sums, count how often each base term occurs.
5. For `sum k r^(k-1)`, align a shifted copy before subtracting.

## 6. INVARIANT_OR_STRUCTURE

- summation is linear;
- nested accumulation can often be reversed into multiplicity weights;
- polynomial kth terms reduce to a small standard-sum basis;
- weighted geometric sums are governed by aligned cancellation after a shift.

## 7. REPRESENTATION_SWITCHES

- visible term pattern -> kth-term polynomial;
- nested sum -> multiplicity-weighted single sum;
- polynomial summand -> split standard sums;
- weighted geometric sum -> original minus shifted copy.

## 8. CONDITION_INDEX_ENDPOINT_CHECKS

- confirm lower/upper sigma bounds;
- verify kth-term expansion at small k before summing;
- nested multiplicity includes the correct first/last occurrence count;
- shift/subtract must preserve the unmatched endpoint term.

## 9. DECISION_BOUNDARIES

- polynomial indexed sequence versus AP/GP classification;
- ordinary standard sum versus nested multiplicity;
- weighted polynomial versus weighted geometric technique;
- expand structurally versus expand every term numerically.

## 10. MISCONCEPTION_TRAPS

`WEIGHTED_SUM_FORCED_TO_AP_GP`, `SUMMAND_NOT_EXPOSED`, `SIGMA_BOUND_ERROR`, `NESTED_MULTIPLICITY_ERROR`, `WEIGHTED_GP_ENDPOINT_ERROR`, `STANDARD_SUM_WRONG_POWER`.

## 11. CONTRAST_PAIRS

1. `sum k(2k+1)` -> polynomial expansion; `sum k 2^(k-1)` -> geometric shift/subtract.
2. `sum_{k=1}^n k` is direct accumulation; `sum_{k=1}^n sum_{j=1}^k 1` first collapses its inner count to k.
3. A list generated by `k(3k+1)` need not be AP/GP to be summable structurally.

## 12. TRANSFER_MECHANISMS

- cumulative triangular arrangements;
- weighted score schedules;
- nested counting where earlier terms recur more often;
- finite geometric weighting with index coefficients;
- polynomial term formula disguised in words.

## 13. SOURCE_CUSTODY
