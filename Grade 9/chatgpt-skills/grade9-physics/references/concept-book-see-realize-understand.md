# Grade 9 Physics Concept Book Protocol

## Purpose

Use this protocol when a Physics concept/reference book must teach *why the physics works*, not merely list definitions and formulas.

Required cognitive sequence:

`SEE THE EQUATION -> REALIZE -> UNDERSTAND -> CONNECT`

`CONNECT` is traceability/navigation. The learning objective remains SEE -> REALIZE -> UNDERSTAND.

## SEE THE EQUATION

The learner encounters the relationship inside something observable or drawable:

- chalkboard sketch;
- motion timeline;
- number pattern;
- graph;
- area model;
- physical demonstration;
- thought experiment;
- before/after comparison;
- two-body or multi-stage diagram.

The equation may appear, but never as an unexplained formula to memorize.

## REALIZE

The learner can explain what every term means physically and can say the relationship in ordinary language.

Example:

`v = u + at`

means:

`final velocity = initial velocity + velocity accumulated because acceleration acts for time t`.

The symbolic relation should now match the physical story.

## UNDERSTAND

The learner can justify or reconstruct the relationship and knows its limits. As applicable include:

- derivation;
- assumptions and model validity;
- coordinate/frame/sign convention;
- dimensional check;
- limiting and special cases;
- graph interpretation;
- proportional/scaling consequences;
- contrast with a plausible wrong idea;
- prediction before calculation;
- reconstruction if the formula is forgotten;
- transfer to differently worded source questions.

## CONNECT

Connect each concept to original/source question IDs, prerequisites, first-step material, question-bank items and challenge/JEE items where applicable. CONNECT must not replace explanation with links.

## Chalkboard choreography

For each major idea prefer:

1. **SEE** — show a physical situation, pattern, graph, table or sketch.
2. **NOTICE** — identify what changes and what stays constant.
3. **SAY** — express the relation in words.
4. **BUILD** — construct the mathematics incrementally.
5. **COMPRESS** — arrive at the compact equation.
6. **CHECK** — units, sign, special case, graph or limiting behavior.
7. **PREDICT** — vary one quantity and predict the consequence before calculation.
8. **RECONSTRUCT** — rebuild the relation from the picture, graph, pattern or earlier law.
9. **CONNECT** — identify source problems unlocked by the idea.

Do not front-load formal derivations before the physical relationship is visible.

## Equation Passport

Every major equation should record:

- equation/relation;
- physical system;
- SEE representation;
- plain-language meaning;
- meaning of every symbol;
- origin of every term, factor, exponent and sign;
- derivation/reconstruction route;
- assumptions and validity;
- units/dimensions;
- graph equivalent where relevant;
- limiting/special cases;
- proportional/scaling consequences;
- common misconception;
- prediction question;
- reconstruction question;
- source question IDs;
- linked first-step/question-bank items.

## Grade 9 depth gate

Accessible explanation does not mean elementary learning depth. Where the concept permits, move beyond the story into several of:

- symbolic derivation;
- algebraic elimination;
- model/assumption selection;
- signed quantities and coordinate choice;
- proportional/inverse-proportional reasoning;
- quadratic or square-root scaling;
- graph/equation/word translation;
- interval versus cumulative quantity;
- multi-stage/two-body reasoning;
- dimensional reasoning;
- limiting-case reasoning;
- reconstruction from first principles;
- transfer to a non-identical problem.

## Mandatory SRU gates

- **SRU-01 No Naked Equation** — no important equation first appears in isolation.
- **SRU-02 Every Symbol Speaks** — symbols need physical meanings, not dictionary labels.
- **SRU-03 Every Term Has an Origin** — explain additive terms, factors, exponents and signs.
- **SRU-04 Explain Unusual Mathematics** — explicitly explain `1/2`, `t^2`, `v^2`, negative signs, square roots and nth-interval differences.
- **SRU-05 Verbalize Before Calculating** — state physical meaning before substitution.
- **SRU-06 Prediction Required** — at least one prediction per major concept.
- **SRU-07 Misconception Confrontation** — use a real misconception, not generic warnings.
- **SRU-08 Reconstruction Test** — learner must be able to rebuild the relation.
- **SRU-09 Source Traceability** — every source question maps to concept sections.
- **SRU-10 Do Not Silently Repair Source Defects** — record missing assumptions, ambiguity, poor notation or ordering defects.
- **SRU-11 Symbolic Depth** — require symbolic reasoning/derivation for major quantitative relations.
- **SRU-12 Assumptions / Validity** — state model conditions.
- **SRU-13 Representation Translation** — translate among at least two of words, diagram, graph, table and equation; three where practical.
- **SRU-14 Scaling Reasoning** — make power/inverse dependence explicit.
- **SRU-15 Transfer** — finish with a non-identical source-style transfer prompt.

## Contrast teaching

Prefer paired examples when similar-looking questions require different modelling choices, such as:

- equal times vs equal distances in average speed;
- stopping time vs stopping distance;
- total distance in 5 s vs distance during the 5th second;
- same speed vs same velocity;
- `v = 0` vs `a = 0`;
- dropped from rest vs released from a moving carrier;
- valid but inefficient equation vs most direct equation.

## Source-grounding workflow

Before authoring:

1. enumerate every source question/item;
2. assign stable concept IDs;
3. identify explicit concept and hidden recognition step;
4. identify governing relation/qualitative law;
5. record source ambiguities/defects;
6. create a Source -> Concept coverage matrix;
7. confirm 100% source coverage before PDF production.

Keep bidirectional traceability:

`source question -> concept section`

and

`concept section -> source questions`.

## Product separation

Concept Book: `SEE -> REALIZE -> UNDERSTAND` — **Why does this work?**

First-Step Reference: `SEE THE STORY -> WRITE WHAT IS KNOWN -> CHOOSE` — **How do I start?**

Question Bank: `RECOGNIZE -> SOLVE -> CHECK -> TRANSFER` — **Can I do it independently?**

## Publication handoff

- preserve board/diagram/equation sequencing;
- do not compress SEE, REALIZE and UNDERSTAND into one dense formula page;
- use a math-capable font stack with complete glyph coverage;
- check superscripts, subscripts, arrows, Greek letters, minus signs, multiplication signs, radicals and alignment;
- reject missing-glyph boxes or fallback-font mismatches;
- keep equations readable at normal A4 print size;
- avoid excessive whitespace that breaks the chalkboard narrative;
- use diagrams for reasoning, not decoration;
- retain concept IDs and source IDs in authoring metadata.

## Definition of done

A concept lesson is complete only when the learner can:

1. recognize the physical situation behind the equation;
2. explain every term in ordinary language;
3. justify/reconstruct the relation;
4. state assumptions and sign conventions;
5. predict scaling or directional consequences;
6. translate among representations;
7. reject a common wrong model;
8. transfer the idea to an unfamiliar source-style question.
