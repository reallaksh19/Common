# Grade 4 English - Adjective Learner Trial Protocol (DRAFT)

**Status:** DRAFT supervised usability/learning check  
**Purpose:** test whether the child-facing adjective routine is independently usable before canonical adoption.

## Trial conditions

Use one Grade 4 learner with an adult observer. The adult may read instructions aloud if normal reading support is required, but must not translate the grammar rule or supply category names.

Use the rendered golden lesson in order, then give one fresh item that the child has not seen in the worked examples.

Record observations, not personality judgments.

## Required probes

1. **Page comprehension:** Ask the child to say what they are supposed to do on a page before starting. Do not paraphrase unless they genuinely cannot proceed.
2. **Classification:** Give a source-supported adjective and ask which book box it belongs to and which question helped.
3. **Boundary meaning:** Show `heavy` or another starred word and ask what the star means.
4. **No invented category:** Ask whether a new box such as `quality` or `condition` is needed. The expected mental model is that the word remains a describing word without creating a new school box.
5. **Placement explanation:** Ask why the starred word appears where it does in the completed phrase. The child should be able to point to the page/model explanation rather than invent a taxonomy rule.
6. **Fresh retry:** Give a new phrase and observe whether the child uses the routine: find noun -> classify -> star extra words -> order -> read aloud.

## Required regression tasks

Include at least:

```text
large heavy brown leather suitcase
broken old metal cooking pot
cheerful new bright classroom posters
```

The fresh retry must use different lexical material from those examples.

## Pass evidence

The trial may be marked `PASS` only when all are observed:

- the child can state the page task without adult grammar translation;
- the child uses the book boxes before reordering;
- a boundary word is not turned into a new school category;
- the child understands that the star means "describing word with no book box";
- the child can explain starred-word placement using the provided model/example language;
- the child completes a fresh retry with the routine and no answer-revealing prompt;
- no internal labels or adult metadata are needed to make the page understandable.

If any item fails, record the exact point of confusion and revise the page/model before rerunning.

## Stop rule

Do not mark canonical adoption ready from one lucky answer. The observer must record the child's actual route through the routine.

Do not infer durable mastery from this usability trial; delayed retention remains a separate evidence stage.
